# Eberron: Common Knowledge Primer
*What “Everyone Knows,” or Thinks They Know*

## A Different Kind of World
Eberron is not a world of ancient ruins waiting to be rediscovered 0 it is a world still recovering from a century of war. Magic is common, practical, and industrialized. Wonders exist not as relics of a lost golden age, but as tools people rely on every day.  Faith is quieter. Heroes are fallible. Evil is rarely simple.  If it help, you can think of it as post WW2 Europe.  

## Sharn & Morgrave University
Sharn, the City of Towers, rises impossibly above the Dagger River—a vertical metropolis of bridges, lifts, and stacked districts where sky-coaches replace horses and the rich literally live above the poor.  There are metaphysical reasons behind it's impossible architecture - Sharn is close to the plane of air, therefore gravity sometimes acts differently in the city.  It is a part of Breland politically.  

Aside from its soaring towers, Sharn is also known as the home of Morgrave University, the most prestigious (and most ethically flexible) center of learning on Khorvaire.  It is a little like Cambridge/Oxford crossed with the British Museum.  It is renowned to possess experts of all stripes and offers learning in a wide range of subjects to any who can pay (mostly the children of noble houses of dragonmarked houses).  However they have a tendency to 'appropriate' rather than 'preserve' which can be a cause for consternation among some academic circles.  They concern themselves with results, not moral philosophy.

Morgrave scholars are respected—and distrusted—throughout the Five Nations.

## The Lightning Rail
The Lightning Rail is the backbone of modern Khorvaire.  They operate similar to railways in our world, but instead of being powered by fuel or electricity, they are powered by *magic*.  Specifically, House Orien use bound elementals to move rapidly across vast distances.  Using lightning rail is far safer than walking or using horses, and more accessible than teleportation magic (which is also predominantly the domain of House Orien, but it not well liked by most of the denizens of Eberron, as it presents a security risk and nations are still rebuilding following the war).

## The Dragonmarked Houses

The Dragonmarked Houses are powerful bloodlines marked by mystical sigils that grant magical abilities tied to commerce, industry, and services. They operate outside national borders, bound by treaty neutrality.  In Eberron, power is often corporate, not royal.  There will be a separate 'cheat' sheet for them!

## The Gods of Eberron

Religion is a frail thing in Eberron.  Gods do not walk the earth, they do not interfere directly in mortal matters.  There will be a separate cheat sheet for them.


## Warforged
Warforged are sentient constructs created by House Cannith for the Last War.  They were used predominantly by Cyre, but other nations did have their own construct soldiers.  They were emancipated by the Treaty of Thronehold (which ended the Last War) and the forges that created the warforged were idled forever.  Now they struggle for identity, purpose and acceptance in a world that views them largely as weapons, and relics of a thing best left in the past.  

### The Lord of Blades
A warforged warlord and messianic figure operating primarily within the Mournland.
* Preaches warforged supremacy
* Rejects coexistence with fleshbound races
* Viewed as a dangerous extremist by most nations

## The Lingering Wound: The Last War & the Mourning
The Last War shaped everything.  The Day of Mourning, which annihilated Cyre, forced the nations of the world to come together and find a way to peace.  That peace, the Treaty of Thronehold, ended the war.  In Eberron, progress and catastrophe walk hand in hand—and no one is entirely sure which will arrive first.
