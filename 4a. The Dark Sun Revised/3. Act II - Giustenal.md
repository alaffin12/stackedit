# Giustenal
The road to New Giustenal is a rough one, but the party should not have much trouble making it with the assistance of Sethara and their kank mounts

#### Sethara
Sethera is the appointed companion of the party, their guide to the sands of Athas.  She is young, but able and experienced.  Her orders are help the strangers so while she may occasionally treat them in a patronizing manner if they do not know how to survive the desert, she is generally somewhat approaching friendly.  If Andromeda has become a Templar Wife she will offer her more respect, but possibly less friendliness - she views Andromeda as a tool in the hands of the Shadow King, just as she is - but a tool she is unfamiliar with

##### History
Sethara was born to no name worth remembering and learned early that survival in Nibenay belonged to the quick and the ruthless. Orphaned young, she grew up in the city’s back alleys and markets, running with a small gang of street children who stole ceramic bits and food to live another day; even then, it was Sethara who led them, deciding when to run, when to fight, and when to vanish. When a younger child under her protection was caught stealing and sentenced to lose his hands, Sethara attacked the guards and failed—then, bloody and desperate, she offered her own hands in his place. As the merchant prepared to take them, Nibenay himself intervened, seeing in her defiance and loyalty a blade worth forging rather than breaking. She was taken into the city’s forces, trained hard and without mercy, and at sixteen was elevated as a Templar Wife of the Chamber of Fire. Now, at twenty-six, Sethara is considered a senior wife within the chamber—respected, feared, and marked by the scar on her left hand, a reminder of the choice that cost her childhood and earned her a place beside a Sorcerer-King.

#### Kanks
Kanks are giant, horse sized ants that are common to the desert.  They have saddles and stirrups, but they will be uncomfortable for the party to ride, and Sethara may snicker a little when they struggle to get on the things.  

## Journey to the City
The journey is going to be largely ornamental, but every day will feature an opportunity for the party to interact with Sethara and learn about Athas and some kind of event.  Both will set the mood and give the party a better feel for Athas.

#### Sethara
Sethara is somewhat brusque when it comes to the party.  She is a professional soldier in the employ of her husband, the Shadow King and that will always shine through.  However she is curious about these strangers (although she has no concept of where they are from - she knows they are from far away, but as an Athasian she has no concept of the planes) and will ask them questions about their homes and history, contrasting it to her own.  Additionally if Andromeda became a Templar Wife she will try and learn about her 
Some questions she might ask include:
* In your cities who controls the water? *Asked because of the importance of water in Athas - she cannot imagine a place where water was not life and death and controlled by the powerful*
* If a child steals food where you come from, what is done to them? *Asked because of her personal history.  Sethara knows the price of crime, even for children in Sethara.  She also knows it made her what she is today.*
* If someone is too weak to travel, do you carry them or leave them behind? *Asked mostly from curiosity - in Athas it would depend on who was too weak to travel and what value they had to you.  She suspects in whatever soft lands the party comes from it would be different.*
* Do people choose your kings where you are from?  Or are they powerful men and women - like the Sorcerer Kings? *Asked because she knows the fate of Tyr - what happens to a city when a powerful sorcerer-king is cast down by the people, and the disaster that followed.* 

She also might ask more personal questions:
* **To Andromeda (not Templar Wife):** I have seen many who borrow power.  I have seen none who refuse a throne and keep it.  Tell me - who do you believe you are answerable to?
* **To Oh:** Where you come from who tells you when to fight?  


### Roc Attack
After a day on the dusty road to Cromlin a flock of Roc will mark the party for a meal...

> For an entire day you ride with Sethara, across the shifting sands, towards another city named Cromlin that you have never once heard of, stopping only when the sun is at its apex, when travelling even on the backs of these insectoid mounts becomes too unbearable for the heat.  Sethara rides easily, one hand on the shaft of her obsidian spear.  She talks quietly, when there is time for talking - answering your questions as best she can, and asking some of her own...

Some questions Sethara might ask:
* *"In your cities, who owns the water?"*
* *"If a child steals food where you come from, what is done to them?"*
* *"When someone is too weak to travel, do you carry them or leave them behind?"*
* *"Do people choose their rulers where you come from?  Or survive them?"*
* *"If your land was dying would your gods save it?"*
* (to Oh) *"You are a soldier.  A mercenary.  When you fight for coin, who decides when you stop being useful.  And once you take the coin do they still know your name?"*
* (to Riric) *"In Nibenay people who survive on the edges learn where the laws do not reach.  Where you come from who hunts you when you cross that line?  And do they hunt with chains, or with favours?"*
* (to Nakos) *"You are one of Dregoth's children."* she shakes her head *"No, I know you are not.  But that is what I see when I see you.  A scar on Athas.  A wound in Nibenay.  Perhaps you are not bound to him as other dray.  But you do kneel to someone.  Tell me - who did you kneel to, and what did those wings cost you?"*
* (to Emilia) *"You carry death like a habit you are trying to unlearn.  You are a soldier, like the other one - Oh - but you hide it much better.  Like you are trying to become something else.  Why?"*
* (to Andromeda if Andromeda did not accept Nibenay's offer) *"You refused my husband's hand in marriage.  That is uncommon.  Few would pass up the safety it offers, and fewer still live to regret their choices very long."* She holds up her hands *"That is not a threat, that is an observation.  I'm sure my husband was clear with you as he was with me - that he does not force himself on his brides.  He requests, and they choose their path.  But in Athas all paths are fraught with dangers and perils.  That is life.  Tell me - how is it that the protection of a king such as Nibenay does not tempt you?"*
* 

Sethara rides easily atop her kank, one hand resting on the shaft of her obsidian spear, her voice steady as the desert wind as she speaks without turning back. She tells them of Athas the way one speaks of a temperamental weapon—how the land itself is always testing you, how cities survive only because something worse waits beyond their walls. She asks questions in return, curious but not reverent: what grows in lands where rain falls freely, whether metal truly lies scattered in the earth like bone, and how wars are fought when water is not the first prize. There is no disbelief in her tone, only the careful interest of someone measuring truths against lived experience.
