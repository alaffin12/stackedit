# Giustenal
The road to New Giustenal is a rough one, but the party should not have much trouble making it with the assistance of Sethara and their kank mounts

#### Sethara
Sethera is the appointed companion of the party, their guide to the sands of Athas.  She is young, but able and experienced.  Her orders are help the strangers so while she may occasionally treat them in a patronizing manner if they do not know how to survive the desert, she is generally somewhat approaching friendly.  If Andromeda has become a Templar Wife she will offer her more respect, but possibly less friendliness - she views Andromeda as a tool in the hands of the Shadow King, just as she is - but a tool she is unfamiliar with

##### History
Sethara was born to no name worth remembering and learned early that survival in Nibenay belonged to the quick and the ruthless. Orphaned young, she grew up in the city’s back alleys and markets, running with a small gang of street children who stole ceramic bits and food to live another day; even then, it was Sethara who led them, deciding when to run, when to fight, and when to vanish. When a younger child under her protection was caught stealing and sentenced to lose his hands, Sethara attacked the guards and failed—then, bloody and desperate, she offered her own hands in his place. As the merchant prepared to take them, Nibenay himself intervened, seeing in her defiance and loyalty a blade worth forging rather than breaking. She was taken into the city’s forces, trained hard and without mercy, and at sixteen was elevated as a Templar Wife of the Chamber of Fire. Now, at twenty-six, Sethara is considered a senior wife within the chamber—respected, feared, and marked by the scar on her left hand, a reminder of the choice that cost her childhood and earned her a place beside a Sorcerer-King.

#### Kanks
Kanks are giant, horse sized ants that are common to the desert.  They have saddles and stirrups, but they will be uncomfortable for the party to ride, and Sethara may snicker a little when they struggle to get on the things.  

## Journey to the City
The journey is going to be largely ornamental, but every day will feature an opportunity for the party to interact with Sethara and learn about Athas and some kind of event.  Both will set the mood and give the party a better feel for Athas.

#### Sethara
Sethara is somewhat brusque when it comes to the party.  She is a professional soldier in the employ of her husband, the Shadow King and that will always shine through.  However she is curious about these strangers (although she has no concept of where they are from - she knows they are from far away, but as an Athasian she has no concept of the planes) and will ask them questions about their homes and history, contrasting it to her own.  Additionally if Andromeda became a Templar Wife she will try and learn about her specifically - they are sisters-in-arms now.  Alternatively, if Andromeda turned down the Shadow King she will be a bit more cool and standoffish towards her, wondering how anyone could turn down such power.  There are a couple of standard responses she will have:

* *If the party reveal metallic weapons/armor* "In your lands... is this common?  Here a thing like that can buy a city's loyalty - or its hatred.  But you carry it as if it were merely a tool."
* *If the party reveals their immense wealth (by Athasian standards)* "My huband told me you were from a land of plenty, but I think he was understating it.  You would need an army to protect so much.  Do you know how many eyes that would buy in Cromlin?"  She shakes her head "Here wealth is not kept.  It is spent - to make allies appear, threats vanish or to keep death away for another day.  Choose which you want before you show that much again."
* *If the party uses divine (elemental) magic* "Here, that kind of power attracts expectations.  Some will see protectors.  Others property.  Still others adversaries.  None will see travellers from a fantastic land."  She shakes her head "Use it when you must.  Hide it when you can.  Not because it is wrong, but because this land does not forgive being reminded that it can be commanded."
* *If the party uses preserving (arcane) magic* "You exhibit control.  Good.  But be warned.  Most here do not know the difference between what you did and what you could have done.  All are taught that such power is a wound waiting to be opened.  Others will see the gesture and feel the fear they have been taught.  If others ask, let me speak.  If others accuse, let me stand between you and them.  I see.  I know.  I have been told.  But Athas has not."
* *If the party uses defiling (arcane) magic* "You take.  You take and take and take and leave nothing but ash." She shakes her head "My husband wields such power.  As do others in his employ.  I will not pretend I have never seen one such as you.  One who defiles.  But it sets me on edge nonetheless.  Understand - I will not stop you.  I will not betray you.  My king - my husband - has commanded me to walk with you.  To accompany you.  I am loyal to him.  Loyal to you.  But right now I would like to see you split open for doing what you just did.  If you must kill this land - if you, from your land of plenty and excess, must steal what little Athas has - then do so.  But do so where no one is left to remember."

### Roc Attack
> As afternoon passes into evening Sethara begins to look up at random times.  She's obviously watching for something.  And then she sees it.  "Dismount...'" she says quietly, quickly "...we are being stalked and the kanks are not trained for battle.  We will lead the hunters away on foot, deal with them and return.  The hunters will follow us, not the kanks - they are very good at hiding in the desert."  She points upwards "Roc.  I count four.  Stay low.  Be careful.  If they carry you upwards it is a long way down."

This can be done theatre of the mind, or it can be done on a blank battle map, since the terrain is generally featureless.  The roc are vicious hunters, and know how to attack.  Starting at about 100 ft they dive bomb the smallest, most exposed target.  In one turn they drop and attack with their claws.  If they hit they carry their prey 20 ft in the air and attack it a second time with their beak.  The next round they move back up to 100 ft and proceed to attack their captured prey with their beak until it dies/breaks free at which point they simply line up for a new bombing run.  If they miss they will attack with their beak and retreat to 20 ft, taking attacks of opportunity to fly away, doing the same attack from 20 ft next round (retreating to 100 ft after the attack).  

Remember falling damage is 1d6 per 10 ft of falling to a maximum of 20d6 (so if players drop from 20 ft they will take 2d6 falling damage; from 100 ft they will take 10d6 falling damage).

### Slaver Attack
The next day the party will be attacked by slavers looking to capture an odd looking band wandering the wastes of Athas - unaware of the fact that the party itself is quite powerful, and they are accompanied by a Templar...

> The sun is still rising in the sky when you come to a fissure in the dune, where sand gives way to stone and splits to create meandering canyons.  Sethara wrinkles her nose.  "We will make our midday camp here.  There will be shade and a breeze.  But keep your eyes up.  This is a good place for an ambush too."

Move everyone to the battle map.  Let them choose where they hide out during the hottest part of the day.  Imply that it matters.  Once everyone is position the Thri-Kreen raiders will position themselves to attack - focusing on the small/weak and those that are isolated.  Thri-kreen targets, and anyone within 15 ft of the targets, get to make a DC 12 Wisdom (Perception) check.  Otherwise they are surprised in the first round.  Then ask them to roll initiative as the Slaver attack commences.

> For a moment you think you hear something.  A strange clicking, like morse code on the desert winds.  Then several large insectoid creatures descend from the high ground towards you.  Sethara grabs her spear.  "Thri-kreen slavers..." she growls to you as she gets to her feet "...don't let them drag you off.  We might never find you out there."  She snarls "Kill the ones with nets first.  And be careful of their poison."

The thri-kreen are looking for slaves.  The raiders will use their nets and try to drag off everyone they can, with the other thri-kreen providing cover.  They won't stick around if they start getting their asses kicked - they'll back off and find easier prey.

### Cromlin
> From the crest of a wind-carved dune, Cromlin reveals itself in fragments—first the strange, pale expanse of the Silt Sea stretching to a hazy horizon, then the scattered shapes of the village clinging to its edge like debris that refused to be swallowed. The ground below shifts from firm sand to fine, drifting powder, and the settlement itself looks temporary, as though a strong enough wind might scatter it. Shacks of bone, driftwood, and scavenged stone lean into one another for support, their surfaces bleached and cracked. A handful of flat, wide skimmers sit half-buried near the shore, tethered to posts driven into the silt. Even at this distance, there is movement—figures passing between structures, pausing, watching. Not welcoming. Never welcoming.
> 
> Sethara brings her kank to a halt and looks down at the town, her expression tightening with distaste and disgust.  Without ceremony she reaches into her pack and pulls out some sun-faded cloth, wrapping it around her shoulders and skirt, hiding them from sight.  She does the same with her spear, wrapping the obsidian tip so that it resembles common stone.  
> 
> *"Cromlin..."* she says, her voice flat *"...where laws go to be forgotten and knives go to be stuck in someone's back."*  Her eyes sweep back and forth, marking the town - searching for threats, even from this distance.  *"There is no sorcerer-king here.  Once upon a time it was fought over between Abalach-Re of Raam and Dregoth himself.  But not any more."*  She sighs slightly *"Here, a Templar is not authority. She is a prize—or a warning. I prefer to be neither.  Stay close.  And try not to look like you belong anywhere else."*
>
> You move down into the town, down among the huts and hovels, the road winding its way along the 'coastline', where the brown sand gives way to grey silt that undulates in the wind in a manner similar - if not identical - to water.    There are docks and jetties here - some ramshackle and new, others dilapidated and failing - and small boats, if they could be called that (although Sethara calls them skimmers).  Beside one is a scaffolding of some sort - perhaps the remains of some kind of crane used to lift heavy goods from a skimmer to shore.  Next to it a small figure - no older than eight or nine - stares up, slowly trying to move up as the tower sways to and fro.  You follow her gaze upwards to see what she is fixated on - near the top you see a fluttering kite, made of sand-blasted red cloth stretched over chitin ribs.  It snaps wildly in the wind, threatening to tear itself apart.  A few nearby adults glance up, but all look away.  No one moves to help.  The young girl slips, stumbles and catches herself as the tower continues to sway

The party can choose to intervene here, or not.  The kite is just a kite, but if they retrieve the young girl's kite the young girl excitedly accepts the kite but runs off.  Frightened of the strangers.  However a few hours later an older lady comes up to the party:

> *"No money..."* she says, in the broken common you have become accustomed to in this land *"...no money.  Can pay with this?"*  She holds up a small trinket, a bracelet with an odd looking periapt hanging from it.  *"Blessed.  Elemental Lords.  All I have."*

The woman is the child's mother, and is offering the charm less as a thank you and more as a means to pay a debt, fearful that the party will demand more from her for saving her child.

In Cromlin the party will stay at a ramshackle inn - called the Siltshade - where accommodations are limited to hammocks and bedrolls.  Sethara will offer to keep watch overnight, but nothing is going to happen - even travelling incognito the party are too well composed and too dangerous looking for common rabble to trouble with.  The most important thing they will do here is decide how to approach Giustenal:

> *"We have to decide now..."* she says, keeping her voice low and her eyes open for any who might listen in *"...well, you have to choose.  I will follow, as I have been bidden.  South of here the land is scarred.  When my husband and the other sorcerer-kings first defeated Dregoth they called on many terrible magics.  They defiled.  The land is scarred, broken.  It will slow us.  It will fight us."*  She pauses and takes a breath.  *"We can hire a skimmer and sail south.  But that presents its own risks.  The Silt Sea can be deadly too.  And finding a skiff-captain to take us south?  It will be expensive.  Finding a good one?  Might be impossible."*

### Route: Silt Sea
If players choose this route allow them to role play moving around Cromlin, looking for a skiff-captain and a worthy vessel.  They have three options:

**Harrak** is a weathered, ash-skinned old captain with pale, clouded eyes.  He will charge the party (110 + 10d6) bits with a percentage discount equal to 15/Charisma (Persuasion) roll.

**Dorek** is a middle-aged, burly captain with dark hair and a perpetual sneer.  He will charge the party (60 + 10d6) bits with a percentage discount equal to 12/Charisma (Persuasion) roll.

**Kethis** is young, with a wide grin and sharp eyes.  He will charge the party (85+10d6) bits with a percentage discount equal to 10/Charisma (Persuasion) roll.

All three get the party close to Giustenal, in the silt-drowned 'estuaries' that border the city, but in some ways you get what you pay for.  Climbing through the estuaries will require a constitution save, with the DC set by how far you have to walk.  Harrak, a seasoned hand at the tiller, gets them pretty close to the walls.  If the party chooses him the DC is set at 12.  Dorek is cheaper, but is far more cautious - he drops them further out into the estuary, and the DC is set at 18.  Kethis is young and brash - he will try to get the party as close as possible, but his recklessness may actually put them further out than even Dorek.  The DC is set at 4d6.  All who fail this save take one level of exhaustion.

#### Silt Horror Encounter
As players make their way from where the skimmer drops them off towards Giustenal they encounter a predator that has adapted itself to the harsh of the Silt Sea.  Have everyone roll a DC 17 Wisdom (Perception) check at disadvantage (as the silt horror stalks them from within the estuary 'rivers'.  Then...

> You pick your way carefully up the uncertain terrain of the estuary.  It is an exercise in patience and care - this silt behaves like water, but is much more deadly.  Each step you take you sink a little, and you hold your breath hoping to make sure you find something solid below.
>
> But you push forward.  One foot after another.
> 
> The something shifts.  It happens in the blink of an eye.  The silt itself erupts, and a monstrous creature emerges.  You cannot tell where it ends and the silt begins - but you do know that it looks hungry.  And it looks at you like food.

The silt horror attacks, and anyone who failed their perception check is surprised in the first round.

##### The Silt
The silt is going to play a major role in this encounter.  There are "rivers" of it stretching across the map, and the silt horror is surrounded by it at all times.  If a player steps in it or starts their turn in it they must make a DC 18 Strength (Athletics) or Dexterity (Acrobatics) check.  If they succeed they may treat the silt as difficult terrain.  If they fail they fall into the silt and are engulfed by it and their turn is complete.  

Being engulfed by silt is dangerous and deadly.  Characters who are engulfed are restrained and blinded.  They also begin to suffocate, but the nature of silt (a light, clay-like sand that clumps when it encounters any moisture at all) makes suffocation more deadly as well.  Characters engulfed in silt can hold their breath for a number of rounds equal to their Constitution modifier + 1 (minimum 1).  Once they can no longer hold their breath they begin to take damage (damage is based on their hit dice - taking a hit die of damage every round).  

Once they are engulfed, at the end of any round (other than the first one) they may make a DC 20 Strength (Athletics) or Dexterity (Acrobatics) check to free themselves from the silt.  

### Route: Tar Pits
The overland route to the south is treacherous, and the party will have to face multiple environmental hazards before finally confronting a Pit Snatcher once they are in sight of Giustenal.

#### The Breathless Hollow
>You come to a place where your path drops through a shallow depression.  As you drop into it you notice the air feels wrong - there's a metallic taste to it.  Sound it muted.  And when you draw breath it feels forced, laborious.

**Option 1:** Push through quickly.  Try and get out of here as soon as possible.  Everyone must make a DC 17 Constitution save.  Those failing take 4d8 necrotic damage.  Those failing by five or more also take a level of exhaustion.  Those succeeding take half damage.

**Option 2:** Move carefully through the depression.  One character makes a DC 18 Intelligence (Arcana) or Wisdom (Survival) check.  On a success they take no damage.  One a failure three characters take 4d8 necrotic damage.  

#### The Silt Storm
>You crest a dune and look out over the path in front of you - barren, lifeless wastes twisted by defiling magic.  Worse still, you see a large, black cloud moving rapidly toward you.  *"Silt Storm..."* Sethara mutters *"...an intense wind that drives the silt of the Silt Sea onto land.  We won't be able to avoid it.  We can seek shelter, or we can try and push through it.  It's your choice."*

**Option 1:** Seek shelter.  DC 16 Constitution Save.  On a fail take 4d6 slashing damage.

**Option 2:** Push through.  DC 18 Dexterity Save.  On a fail take 6d6 slashing damage.  On a fail of five or more take a level of exhaustion.   

#### The Dim Sun
> You push onwards, towards your destination.  The world around you feels heavy and as you look up you notice the sun dimming - like a shroud has been cast over it.  In it's light you see an image projected onto the sands: seven beings of power - such power that they radiate it like the sun, making them hard to look at - approach an eighth, who is sleeping.  Or resting, perhaps - does a being of such power even sleep.  He wakens, and you can feel it as the seven begin to tear him apart with power.  They strike him with everything they have.  And more.  You feel them tear power from the land itself.  From you even.  You feel your energy being drawn in.  You see lightning.  Nibenay summoning white hot flames that wreathe the lone figure.  A sword of ebony.  You try to hold on.  To fight the power being drawn from you

No options here.  Everyone makes a DC 17 Wisdom save.  Mazirak has advantage (legacy of combat with mind flayers).  Emelia has advantage (psionic training).  Andromeda has advantage if she chose to become a Templar Wife (protection of Nibenay).  Those who fail take 4d8 psychic damage.  If they fail by 5 or more they also lose a healing die.

> The image fades as the lone figure is struck down.  The sun glows brightly again, beating down on you once more.

#### Arcane Static Front

> You crest a ridge and notice a shimmering wave of arcane energies snaking rapidly towards you.  Those of you who are arcane spell-casters feel a weight in your mind, as your spells are pulled toward this energy.  You realize you can struggle to hold on to your own magic, or you can try to let go and ride the proverbial wave... for better or worse.

Arcane spellcasters (Mazerak, Nakos, Andromeda) have to choose to resist or let go.  If they resist they must make a DC 18 Spellcasting ability check.  If they fail they lose their highest level spell slot.  If they fail by five more more they also take 4d10 force damage.  If they let go their magic loses some of its potency - their spell attacks and their spell save DCs have a -1 penalty until they complete their next long rest.  

#### Tar Pits
> You move into an area where the desert descends into little slot canyons that wend through through the desert in labyrinthine fashion.  The canyons are dotted with black pits filled with something akin to tar.  
> 
> *"The Blood of Athas..."* Sethara mutters *"...or tears maybe.  Two thousand years ago the sorcerer-kings attacked one of their own.  The one we now hunt.  My husband says Dregoth had gotten too powerful, that he had become too close to becoming another Borys.  Borys was a necessity - something powerful enough to keep Rajaat imprisoned - but the other sorcerer-kings had no such need for Dregoth.  They brought all their power to bear against him.  The power of the Elemental Lords, the power of the Way, of strength of arms and of defiling."*  She looks at one of the pits and sniffs *"It left scars.  Be wary here - those pits are not natural.  They are the act of defiling made manifest."*

The players can move around the map.  When they reach choke points they will have to decide what order they go through.  Most times it will not matter, but when they go through the choke point marked "A" they will be attacked from both sides - with a tar wraith and a tar thrall appearing in front of them and three tar thralls appearing behind them.  

##### The Tar
The tar pits themselves are going to be a major factor in this battle.  They have the following characteristics:
* If a creature enters a tar pit it must make a DC 18 Strength save.  If they succeed, they may move through the tar as if it were difficult terrain.  If they fail their speed drops to zero until the start of their next turn.  If they fail by five or more they fall into tar, and are engulfed by it.
* If a creature starts its turn in the tar it takes 2d10 necrotic damage.  They must make a DC 18 Strength save.  If they succeed they may move through the tar as if it were difficult terrain.  If they fail they fall into the tar, and are engulfed by it.
* Creatures engulfed by the tar are blinded, restrained and prone.  At the start of their turn they must make a DC 17 Constitution save.  If they fail they take 4d10 necrotic damage and gain a level of exhaustion.  If they succeed they take half damage and are not exhausted.  At the end of their turn they may make a DC 18 Strength saving throw.  If they succeed they push themselves up from the mire and are no longer engulfed by the tar.
* If an arcane spell of third level is cast within 30 ft of a tar pit roll a d6.  On a 1-2 a random creature within 20 ft. of a pit is pulled 10 ft towards the pit.  On a 3-4 the next tar creature that makes an attack does do at advantage.  On a 5 the spell caster take 2d10 necrotic damage.  On a 6 roll twice more and both happen.

## Into Giustenal
### Sighting Giustenal
> From a distance Giustenal is a mighty walled city on the banks of a silt river tributary that flows into the Silt Sea.  You can imagine why it was built - it was once a bustling port city, filled with trade and silt-skimmers coming and going.  As you draw close though you see the truth.  The city was built on a rise and then follows the rise as it sinks down towards the silt.  Perhaps the Sorcerer-King who once called this city his kept the silt at bay in his time.  But now the silt rises, having been blown into the lower parts of the city, drowning all beneath it.  
>
> Sethara eyes the situation dismally.  "The walls of Giustenal stand, even after two millennia.  We can try the gates, but is Dregoth is active he will certainly have a watch on them.  Dray, most likely..." She gestures to Nakos "...like him.   We can try to scale the walls, but it is tricky enough to scale a wall when it is in good condition - and these walls do not look like they are in good condition,"  She shakes her head.  "The choice falls to you.  I will follow where you lead."

### Entrances
There are three potential entrances into Giustenal - either by going over a ruined section of the western wall, entering via the south gate or entering via the eastern gate.  

#### Through the Gates
Entering via the gates leads to an encounter with a patrol who are guarding the entrance (one Dray Way Mind, four Dray Wardens and six Bone Sentinels).  If the battle starts to go against the Dray (which is probably will) then the Way Mind will silently command one of the Dray Wardens to retreat and raise an alarm.

#### Over the Wall
Going over the wall seems pretty easy at first - the walls are older and there are lots of good handholds, so it only requires a Strength (Athletics) check of DC 10 to scale the first 30 ft.  However near the top that advantage turns to a hindrance, as the wall is in such bad condition that hand holds spontaneously crumble in the climbers hands.  Players must make a DC 20 Strength (Athletics) check to complete the climb and make it to the top.  If a PC fails their check by 5 or more they fall from the wall, taking 3d6 falling damage.  Additionally, part of the wall falls on top of them, forcing them to make a DC 18 Dexterity save or take an additional 5d6 bludgeoning damage.

### Inside Giustenal
Once inside Giustenal players are going to have to move around the ruins without attracting too much attention.  Giustenal has an alarm level, which ranges from 0 to 5.  If the party entered by the wall they start at 0.  If they entered via one of the gates they start at 1.  

Every time they move to a new area of the city they have to make a group Dexterity (Stealth) check vs DC 13 + Alarm Level.  If less than half the party make their stealth check, the alarm level goes up by one.  If all of the party make their check, the level drops by one.  

Players may also try to reduce their alarm level.  To do this they make a group Dexterity (Stealth) check against the same DC for moving.  If 50% or more of the party make their check the alarm level drops by one.  If they do not reach the 50% threshold the alarm level goes up by one.

#### Alarm Levels and Consequences
##### Level 0-1 (Sleeping Ruins)
The servitors of Dregoth are mostly ignorant to the party's presence.  They draw no attention to speak of.  Moving through zones is mostly safe.

##### Level 2-4 (Stirring Watch)
The party has attracted the attention of Dregoth's forces.  Moving from zone to zone forces an encounter from the **Quick Event** table.

##### Level 5 (Active Hunt)
The party are being actively hunted by Dregoth's forces.  They still generate Quick Events as if the watch was stirring.  Further, if they would raise the alarm level once they are at level five they instead trigger the "Desert Ruins" encounter.

#### Quick Events
Quick events centre around checks and the consequences of failing those checks.  Some are group checks, where if 50% of the party (not including Sethara) make their save/check the entire party avoids the consequences (however if fewer than 50% make it then the entire party suffers the consequence).  Others are individual checks, where the consequence applies to the individuals who fail the checks.  DC's are based on the alarm level and are equal to 11+the Alarm Level.  For non-magical characters with other expendable resources, a barbarian rage counts as a level 3 spell.  A psionic die counts as a level 1 spell.

#### Desert Ruins Encounter
> You cut down alleys, trying to avoid Dregoth's forces and move towards your final goal - the sorcerer-king himself.  But it is difficult.  The city is swarming with his dray, dragonborn who are all too eager to serve him. 
>
> You manage to find a sunken road that cuts through this neighbourhood in the direction you are travelling.  It's good - it keeps you low and out of sight, but soon you find you are trapped on it - moving only in the directions it allows you to move, channeling you to its end with too few exits.  Sethara warns you that it is a prime spot for an ambush.

Track damage done and spells/resources used in this encounter (remember - rages count as three spell slots, psionic die count as one).  If the party triggers it again rather than run the same combat again the build the cost around these numbers.

##### The Noble's Quarter

**The Mirror Test**
This room was once a loyalty test used by Dregoth to ensure the obedience of the nobility.  The loyal would submit to the power of the mirrors and kneel before him willingly, receiving a 'boon'.  The disloyal would resist and be punished.

Players can resist the mirrors a number of ways.  Dragonborn characters have advantage on all their saves.  If the player chooses to simply resist the power of the mirrors they must make a DC 22 Wisdom Save or fail.  However make it clear that the mirrors are not omniscient - they do not see all and can be defeated by clever thinking instead of brute will.  Some possible solutions (with DC's):

* Charisma (Persuasion/Intimidation): Force the mirror to see reality as you see it.  (DC 18)
* Wisdom (Insight): See the falsehood of the images, use that to ground your resistance to their influence (DC 17)
* Intelligence (Arcana): Grasps the magic behind the mirrors, use it to disrupt the spell (DC 16)
* Charisma (Deception): Trick the mirror into seeing your loyalty to Dregoth.  (DC 16)

If a player succeeds they can regain a hit die, or hit points equivalent to a hit die if they prefer.  Alternatively they may also regain one level one spell slot, or some other equivalent resources.

If a player fails they take 1d8 psionic damage and gain a dray taint (dragonborn characters are immune to this) until their next long rest.  The following taints exist:
