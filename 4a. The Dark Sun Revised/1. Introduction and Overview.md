> My mind turns curiously time and again to Athas, the Land of the Dark Sun.
>  
> I have not ventured often to Athas.  It is a grim place, one that has fallen out of balance with itself.  And magic - my weapon and ally - is problematic there.  But still, as I search for the foundational secrets of the cosmos I think of that blasted, desert world.  A stubborn world, holding on against its own collapse.  But why is it collapsing?  One might point to the defiling magic wielded by its sorcerer-kings, or the scorching heat of its dying sun.  They might even point to Raajat Warbringer, a figure from their distant history who chained the world.  But those answers are short, limited.  Yes - defiling magic is behind the dying planet and perhaps even its dying sun.  Yes - Raajat introduced defiling magic to the world.  But why has magic failed in Athas?  Not just magic.  Why have the gods abandoned Athas?  What happened?  I suspect the answer lies back in the depths of its history - back in what the locals call the Blue Age some fourteen thousand years prior.  But how does one even look for something that happened in the ancient past, in an age forgotten and reviled and dreamed of?

**NOTE:** Before the party leaves for Athas find out exactly what coins they are bringing and what equipment they are bringing.  Unlike other settings, where things can be hand-waved away, in the blasted land of Athas scarcity will make a massive difference.  That which is not brought cannot necessarily be easily replaced.

## Outline
Athas is a grim and dark fantasy setting, one where magic is the deadly domain of the sorcerer kings and psionics is the order of the day.  Keep the following in mind in Athas:

### Arcane Magic - Defiler & Preserver
The flow of magic has been disrupted in Athas, and it is difficult to use even by normally powerful arcane spell-casters.  Defilers are arcane spell-casters who take the easy way out, and draw the power for their magic from the world around them.  This is powerful, but it scars the land - leaving it barren and lifeless.  Preservers, on the other hand, try to maintain the balance of magic - often sacrificing power in the name of a grander purpose.

#### Basics
Each time an arcane spell-caster casts a levelled spell (cantrips do not count), they must choose if they are defiling or preserving and roll 1d20.

#### Defiling Magic
If the spell-caster chooses to defile and they roll higher than 10 on their 1d20 roll they cast the spell without consuming a spell slot.  Using defiling magic causes obvious visual cues as the land around the caster turns blackened and lifeless.  The defiler also taints themselves with their fell magic - track the number of times they defile and apply the following:
* For 5+ 'defiles' the defilers max HP is reduced by 1 per 'defile'.
* For 10+ 'defiles' the defiler cannot regain HP for 1 hour after defiling.
* For 15+ 'defiles' the defiler begins to mutate, exhibiting physical signs of the fell magic they use, and will have disadvantage on all charisma check with anyone in Athas not aligned with defilers.
Spell-casters reduce their number of defiles by 1 every long rest.  

#### Preserving Magic
If the spell-caster chooses to preserve and they roll a 1 their 1d20 the spell fizzles and the spell slot is consumed.  If they roll a 2 or a 3 they trigger a wild magic effect (see table below).

### Divine Magic - The Gods of Athas
There are no gods.  That does not mean that divine spellcasters are out of luck - their connection to their gods is deep enough that they will still be able to make contact.  But Athas changes their magic too.

The first time a divine spell-caster casts a spell ask them which of the four classical elements they feel the strongest affinity for in the moment.  Then consult the table below:

| Spell Type | Affinity | Opposed | Neutral 1 | Neutral 2 |
| -- | -- | -- | -- | -- |
| **Healing** | **WATER** – Spell restores +1 HP per spell level or ends 1 extra condition if applicable. | **FIRE** – Healing reduced by 1 HP per spell level (min 1); flames flicker across target’s skin painfully | **EARTH** – The healed area gains barklike texture; skin hardens, briefly | **AIR** – Target exhales mist or fog; healing feels “light” and distant |
| **Protection** | **EARTH** – Target gains +1 to Strength saves for 1 minute or resistance to being shoved | **AIR** – Target’s hair floats, aura feels unstable; Bless/Shield of Faith may flicker for a round (disadvantage on 1 concentration save) | **WATER** – Buff feels "soothing"; the target feels calm or sedated | **FIRE** – Buff feels hot and surging; target has glowing veins, drawing attention |
| **Damage** | **FIRE** – +1 damage per die for spells dealing fire, radiant, or necrotic damage (only 1 spell per round) | **WATER** – First damage die is halved (min 1); wet air or rain-like sizzle mutes the attack | **AIR** – Attack surges forward with a gust or whistling sound | **EARTH** – Ground trembles, rocks erupt around target (harmlessly) |
| **Divination** | **AIR** – Gain advantage on 1 Int or Wis check related to the spell’s purpose (e.g., Insight, Perception, Arcana) | **EARTH** – Spell has a 1-round delay (you learn info on next turn); or you get only partial answer at first | **WATER** – Images appear as ripples in water or reflected light | **FIRE** – Sparks leap around your eyes; info received feels overwhelming or painful briefly 

Initially players will be able to choose their element, but eventually the DM will choose an element they seem to have a strong affinity with and will make them stay with it.

### Secrets Revealed
Eventually the party is going to find out the following:
During the Blue Age the Rhulisti Halflings desired to improve their paradise.  Masters of magic, psionics and nature they turned to the Ritual of Remaking to build a "better" world.  But the ritual went awry, and their reality began to consume itself - the Brown Tide was the most obvious outward expression of the impending collapse, but there were others.  Ostensibly this collapse was because the ritual had impacted a core part of the existence of Athas, but while the Rhulisti knew enough about the ritual to know this they could not recall that had been lost - it was simply erased from the collective consciousness.  

The Rhulisti realized that to stave off the collapse of their reality they needed a new 'core secret'.  They built the Pristine Tower to serve as that 'core secret'.  It stemmed the tide, but the cost was great.  It was not a true secret, so it required massive amount of energy - the life force of the entire planet - to sustain it.

Generations later Raajat followed in the footsteps of the Rhulisti.  He became aware of what the Rhulisti had tried to accomplish, and decided he would correct their mistakes and complete their plan - restoring Athas to its former glory.  His efforts to do so destabilized the Pristine Tower, leading to the environmental collapse of Athas and forcing (most of) his champions, led by Borys, to imprison him.  

Vecna came to the Pristine Tower alone to learn the secrets of the Rhulisti, and how to accomplish what they could not.  He left with his secret knowledge, but has kept an eye on it since then, knowing the secrets there could defeat him.  When the party reveal themselves Vecna will come and Athas will shake, and the party will have to make a decision - flee Athas, leaving behind the secret knowledge they were searching for (and weakening them in their final battle against Vecna); or unleash Raajat on Vecna, knowing that it will mean the utter end of Athas.
