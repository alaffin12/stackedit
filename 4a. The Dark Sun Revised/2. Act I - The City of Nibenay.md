# Nibenay
## Arrival
> You do not so much arrive in the city as it collapses into place around you.  You step through the boundary between world, through endless void, and into a narrow back alley - hemmed in by narrow stone walls that lean inwards above you.  Beneath your feet the ground is not soil, nor stone, but dust - powdered and compacted and shaped into a foot-path, yet ground so fine that each step coats your boots with grey and hangs gently in the air, like a cloud.
>
> The air!  It is hot, but it is not the clean heat of summer.  It is not even the humid warmth of the jungle.  The heat is baked it.  It is oppressive, laying upon you like a weight.  It feels as if the city itself has been cooking, trapped in an over for centuries and never cooled.  Every breath feels dry, rasping and faintly acrid.
> 
> The is no birdsong here.  No insects buzzing.  No distant wind rustling through the leaves.  There is a constant low murmur.  The scrape of sandals on dirt, the clink of bone and ceramic and the hiss of whispered bargains and secrets.  The city - like all cities - is alive.  But it does not sing.  It grinds.  
>
> You look up at the narrow rays of the sun, slicing into the alleyway.  You see walls of dark, smooth stone, carved with intricate, almost hypnotic patterns that serpentine back and forth around itself.  The light shimmers near the wall, and you know the stones absorb heat.  You would burn yourself, if you could touch them.
>
> A pair of locals pass by the mouth of the alley.  They do not stop.  They do not stare.  The avoid all eye contact and keep their heads down, robes pulled tight against the sun.  But you know they have seen you.  Marked your arrival, though they know not what it means.
>
> You step out of the alley, into the streets of the city, and move around for a bit.  The people here do not speak common, and you do not speak whatever language they do.  It isn't long before someone comes up to you, speaking quickly in the strange language of the locals, making gestures as if urgently trying to get you to move.  Eventually she sighs softly, as if she is confirming something she already suspected.  
>
>*"You... not from here."*  It's not so much a question as it is a declaration.  *"You look wrong.  Walk wrong.  This city, it eat people who walk like you."*  She shakes her head *"This alley.  Not allowed.  Wives only.  You understand?  Big trouble.  Wives come and take you."*  She looks at you carefully, sizing you up, evaluating you.  Her common might be broken and halting, but the look your recognize.  It is the same look a merchant gives when you ask to buy from their special stock, their private collection.  She's trying to decide if you are worth her time.  Eventually she decides you are.  *"Tzani help you?  Tzani knows streets.  Knows rules.  Know who hurt you if you break rules.  You want water?  Want place to sleep where knife not come in night?  Tzani show."*  She pauses *"Not free.  Tzani need bits too.  Need food, need water too.  First lesson free.  Do not go down alley.  Second lesson need pay."*

### Tzani
Tzani of the Black Canals is a commoner in Nibenay.  She appears to the party wrapped in layered robes the colour of baked clay, her face half-hidden beneath a hood pulled low against the sun.  Her dark brown eyes are sharp - watching everything, evaluating everything.  They are lean, scarred and utterly at ease in the heat.  Her primary purpose is to give the party a small introduction to Nibenay and Athas before the adventure kicks off.  Below are a few 'pre-scripted' reactions to certain 'we don't know Athas' actions the party might take:

#### Coins
When the party produces off-world coin, Tzani’s reaction should be **immediate, visceral, and controlled with effort**. At the sight of a **copper piece**, she goes still, eyes flicking instinctively to the street before she speaks; her voice drops and sharpens, and she does not touch it:

> *“You show that too loud."* Tzani mutters, pushing you coin back into your hand *“That buy house. Maybe two. People die for less."*  She looks at you for a moment, measuring and evaluating you again.  *"Rich fool you are.  Showing off?  I care not.  But pay me in coin that not get me killed.  I know man - he change copper for bits.  For fat merchants.  He crook.  Pay you seven when it worth ten.  But you give him one or two.  He give you seven or fourteen, you give me one.  I am crook too.  Too much for me.  But you are fool."*  

If they offer **silver**, her composure cracks — not into greed, but fear.  

> Tzani steps back, makes a warding sign, and hisses, *“No. No, you hide that. That Templar money.  That noble house money.  For me - for you - that curse. Wives see, they ask why you have.  They ask where you find.  Then they kill you for it and go find more.”* 

A **gold piece** is unthinkable: she refuses it outright, believing it impossible.

> Tzani stares at you, her face pale with anger and alarm, whispering disbelievingly *"That... that gold?  Put away. Now. That not from this world. Nothing here should shine like that. You carry years that do not exist.”* 

In every case, she insists on payment in ceramic instead, not because she doesn’t want the wealth, but because she has no way to spend it, to use it for anything except bringing bad things to her door.

#### Metal
Unlike coins, which are small and easily hidden, metal equipment is harder to hide.  Presumably cloaks, tabards and the like will obscure it briefly, but eventually Tzani will catch a glimpse of it.  Her reaction is similar to when they showed the coins, but sharper.  

> Tzani comes to a complete halt, like savanna creature who has just seen the predator raise its head from the watering hold.  She looks at you all long and hard before practically pushing you off to one side and standing between you and the open street.  *"No..."* she says firmly, any softness in her voice gone.  She gestures to the hint of metal, but sees more - her eyes going wide, muttering in a strange language.  Her expression is one of bewilderment, of confusion.  Of fear.  *"Hide.  Wrap.  Cover..."* she mutters, suddenly trying to hide every hint of metal from the light *"...wives see, they take.  Take you too.  Ask where from.  Then ask who saw."* She stares at you, her expression dark.  Her meaning is clear.  *"Me.  I saw."*  She emphasizes it anyway, then shakes her head.  *"Only king's dogs and fools wear sun-steel.  You are not dogs.  So you must stop being fool!"*  

As with coins Tzani's main concern is not that the players have steel weapons/armor.  It's that they shouldn't.  And that means trouble for them - trouble that could wend its way down to her.

#### Divine Magic
Divine magic is not unheard of in Athas, but it is different - and Tzani will notice the differences immediately and react...

> Tzani hesitates, watching you carefully.  You see her eyes casting about, looking for something.  Some sign, something familiar.  Failing to find it she rounds on you.  *"No circle?  No vow?  No price?  No signs from flame or wind or earth or water?"*  She shakes her head. *"People who take power from nowhere - they watched.  By king.  By something else.  Athas not give gifts.  Athas trades.  Athas takes.  If you not give, then who?"*  

After seeing a display like this Tzani is afraid.  Not of the players, exactly, but what they represent.  Something different, something powerful.

#### Psionic Power
Everyone has psionic power in Athas, so unlike divine magic (or arcane magic) Tzani has a reference point at least.  She understands.  

> Tzani looks at you and shakes her head.  *"Too loud..."* she says, gesturing to you as if you were shouting *"...you strong, but you loud.  Push hard.  Everyone hear little.  But you shout.  Sign without sigil.  Without glaze.  That make questions."*

#### Arcane Magic
> Tzani is shocked as you conjure up the elements of arcane magic in front of her, her eyes wide with fear and hate.  *"No!"*  Her voice is a whispered shout, full of fear and anger.  *"You burn world!"* **If the magic is preserving:** *"You say you careful.  All say that.  Land still die."* **If the magic is defiling:** *"You kill it!"* she spits *"You hear it scream?  Skies take you.  Sun burn you."*  She spits in the ground at your feet. *"Kings are monsters.  No choice for them.  You?  You choose.  Curse you, you choose!"*  With that final pronouncement she moves away, fleeing you.  She reports the party to the Templars after she flees, setting up a confrontation shortly after.

### Tour
Tzani will take the party on a 'tour' of the city.  Depending on how the party reacts this can be as long or as short as it needs to be, but the basic idea is to give a rough introduction to Athas, to set the stage for the players and give them a feel for Athas.

#### The Money-Changer: Hassir of the Broken Wheel
> Tzani guides you to a narrow stone building.  It's a simple design - there is a small niche in the stone, and inside the niche a single window barred with a mix of wood and stone.  A shingle hangs above the niche, advertising the place to those who can read the strange language of this land **(the sign says: Money Changer)**.  *"He rob you..."* Tzani says *"...but he rob quiet.  Better for you.  Fair price means questions.  You pay more for no questions."*

Hassir is a quiet man.  He doesn't talk much, and when he does it is typically in Nibenese.  If the players show they do not understand him he will half-sigh and speak to them in broken common, usually in one word phrases (think like "coin" while pointing to the counter to get them to put their coins on the counter, or "here" when he slides the ceramic pieces and bits across the counter to them).  He does not touch the copper coins himself - rather he uses a pair of tongs (this keeps people from claiming he has stolen from them - he cannot vanish a coin using slight of hand with tongs) weighing out each coin before dispensing ceramic pieces and bits.  The exchange rate is simple here: six pieces and ten bits for each copper piece.  There is no room for negotiation.  If the players try to sell silver or gold to him he will shake his head.  If the party understands Nibenese he will say: *"those coins shine nice, but they are not copper.  I do not deal in false metals.  Only copper"*.  Otherwise Tzani will tell them: *"He say he no know those coins, he not trade.  You fool for showing him.  He know coin, he send wives to take us.  Or thieves.  No spit which one."* (In actual fact Hassir does know the coin and he does tell the Templars, setting up a confrontation with the Templars).

#### The Water Court
> Tzani leads you into an open plaza, marked by cracked stone and what appears to be several large cisterns.  The entire square is monitored by eight women, stationed in at various positions around the square, each wearing a similar uniform - a yellow skirt and black armor made from what would appear to be the carapace of dead insects on the top as well a a bone helmet.  Each carries a long spear tipped with some kind of black spear-point - perhaps made from the same material as their armor, or perhaps obsidian.  *"This the water court..."* Tzani whispers *"...Water wives watch.  Slaves work.  Rules here strict.  Water is law.  Do not argue.  Do not drink before pay."* She shakes her head *"Do not let see how thirsty.  Charge you extra."*

Water is sold here at the cost of one bit per cup.  Slaves dole out the water, some lugging it in large jugs from down in the cisterns, others pour cups of water and still others collect payment and record amounts.  This is the first time the party will have seen all of the races of Nibenay and it is surprisingly cosmopolitan - most of the figures in the lines wears shrouds that cover their faces and bodies, but the slaves as not so protected - they see goliaths (called half-giants), giant bug creatures (called thri-keen) and short hairless dwarves (called Mul - actually half-dwarves) carrying water while other races like humans and elves do the book keeping.  Lines are long here, but they move fast.  

> As you shuffle through the line you see a commotion near the front.  A man near the front of the line suddenly lunges forward.  He barrels into an elf carrying a tray of cups, knocking him hard onto the stone.  Water sloshes, cups clatter and fall, spilling the precious liquid into the dust where it vanishes instantly.  He speaks in Nibenese - almost babbling, certainly desperate, and grabs as many cups as he can from the tray.  
> 
> Before you can even react the armoured women in the square move.  There is no haste to their actions - they do not run, they do not push.  They simply stride, and the crowd parts for them.  One raises a hand and the thief tumbles to the ground, his muscles suddenly locked by invisible chains, his ill-gotten gains tumbling out of his hands and spilling into the dust.  Another says a single word - not one you understand, but one with power.  It is spoken calmly, but forcefully.  A command.  And the air around the man answers.  It lifts him from the ground into the air, and then slams him back down again.  You hear bones break as he lands.  
> 
> *"Thief..."* Tzani mutters *"...fool.  Take water with wives here.  Stupid.  Insult."*
> 
> The man slowly tries to get to his feet, but the invisible chains still bind him.  And his ribs are broken from the force of his lands.  He cries out as the armoured women lace their arms in his and drag him to his feet and babbles some more.  One of the women strikes him across the face.  *"He say child dry.  Child die without water.  Wife not care.  City not care."*
> 
> The elf, meanwhile, has prostrated himself on the ground, staying where he fell, forehead pressed to the ground.  One of the armed women walks over to him and snaps her fingers.  He scrambles quickly to his feet and bows before being struck by the woman.  Tzani shakes her head.  *"He punished later.  Not now.  Court business first."*  She exhales slowly, before gesturing to the party.  *"This Nibenay strangers.  Water move.  People break.  City stand."*

#### The Bone Market
> Tzania leads you into a busy market square.  It is alive with sounds and movement, buzzing with tongues and wind and feet shuffling in the sand.  Tzania lets you take it all in for a moment, almost proud of it - as if to say here is the greatest market square in all the land.  It is small by the standards of other worlds (and in truth, it might not even be the grandest in Athas even) but Tzania does not know that.  Her world does not extend much beyond the walls of this city.
>
> *"Come..."* she says after giving you a moment *"...to Kel.  Kel keep mouth closed.  Help with shiny problem.  Kel expensive.  Pay for no questions."*  She gestures to a small stall where you see small bites of food roasting above a small brazier.  *"If hungry, eat.  Okay?"*

##### Equipment
Most of the stalls sell things of little interest to the party - textiles, parts for wagons and bone tools.  There are places that sell bone and stone weapons if the players want, but Tzania will try and urge them to go to Kel's before they go on a shopping spree.  

##### Kel's Bone & Skin
> The place where Tzania leads you is not a stall, but a low stone building on the edge of the marketplace.  It is not particularly notable other than the shingle hung outside, written in a language you do not understand (if they can read Nibenese it says "Kel's Bone & Skin".  
> 
> Tzania leads you inside, which is a single room - perhaps with a couple smaller rooms off the back of the place.  The room smells of resin, bone and cured hides, and a variety of tools are hung on the walls and scattered around the room.  An older human sits at a table, shaping a bone knife.  His hands are rough and scarred, but steady.
>
> Tzania and he exchange words in Nibenese - a brief, terse conversation, before Tzania turns to you.  *"Kel help.  Make dangerous things look boring.  That is expensive skill.  Understand?  Now show him what you need hidden"*  

If the players can understand Nibenese and want to know what Tzania and Kel say to one another.

> **Kel:** Ah, my favorite troublemaker.  What brings you to darken my door.
> **Tzania:** Coin Kel.  Your favorite.  My new friends have need of your services.
> **Kel:** Your new friends anything like the last ones you brought to see me?  Thieves with big mouths that needed keys made?.
> **Tzania:** No.  Better work.  Nothing illegal.
> **Kel:** Nothing illegal.  Right.  Then why bring them to me.
> **Tzania:** Okay, look - its on the edge of illegal.  These strangers - they have coin.  Not bits.  Real coin.  They have steel.  Real steel Kel.
>  **Kel:** Steel?  Bullshit.  No one has steel except the Templar-Wives and the Sorcerer-King.
>  **Tzania:** They do Kel.  Swear to the skies.  That's why we're here.  Because if they wear steel out for all to see there are going to be questions.  So they want you to hide it.  Now stop asking questions before you hear an answer that gets you in trouble.
>  **Kel:** Do they talk, or at they all mutes?
>  **Tzania:** Only Tyran.  And bad Tyran too.  Worse than mine.  It's hard to understand them sometimes.
>  **Kel:** Tyr eh?  Didn't figure you'd be working with Tyrran scum.
>  ** Tzania:** Clean the wax from out of your ears Kel.  They're not Tyrran.  At least they don't sound like they are.  Bottom line is they are strangers and that have coin and they need your services.  Now do you want to get paid or not?
>  **Kel:** uine.  Tell them to show me what they need covered.

Kel charges the following rates: 25 bits for a small metal weapon; 40 bits for a medium metal weapons; 15 bits to hide buckles, and other small metal fittings; 60 bits for light armour; 90 bits for medium armour; 150 bits for heavy armour.  Weapons and armour that are so disguised have a -1 penalty to attack and AC rolls.  Additionally, if an attack rolls a natural 1 the disguise cracks and the weapon now critical fails on a natural 1 or 2.  Alternatively if someone wearing disguised armor is hit with a critical hit, the armours disguise is damages and the AC penalty is increased to -2.  These can be repaired during a long rest.

#### The Temple
> You step out from the smaller streets onto a broad avenue that seems to surround a structure in the centre of the city.  On the other side of roadway is a wall, taller than the walls around outside of the city, with a massive gate.  Behind that you see what can best be described as a massive stone mountain - with a broad base and terraced levels, each side stepping inwards as it climbs.  Each tier is edged with low walls and shallow stairs, all worn smooth by centuries of feet.  From a distance it looks less like a building and more like something grown - a rigid, vertical spine of stone erupting from the earth.  
>
>At its heart towers a narrow, soaring spire, square at the base but tightening as it rises, until it ends in a blunt, crown-like peak. The stone is dark and weathered, carved everywhere with reliefs: processions of slaves carrying jugs, templars in ritual poses, kings crowned by the sun. The figures overlap and repeat, as if the stone itself is telling the same story again and again.
>
>The temple’s walls are pierced by deep doorways rather than windows. Each entrance is framed by massive stone lintels carved with coiling serpents, burning suns, and stylized faces with empty eyes. The doorways feel less like invitations and more like thresholds - places you pass through knowing you will not come back unchanged.
>
>Water runs everywhere. Narrow stone channels guide it down the terraces, spilling from carved mouths and basins into shallow pools where slaves kneel to fill jugs. The air is cooler here, heavy with damp stone, incense, and the faint metallic scent of water that has touched too many hands.
>
>At the very top, far above the noise of the city, the spire looms — not wide enough to be climbed by many at once, but impossibly tall. From below, it gives the unsettling impression that the temple is watching, that the city itself is arranged around this place like an offering.
>
> *"Naggaramakam"* Tzania says *"Spine of city.  Home of Nibenay.  Water.  Law.  Templars.  All from here."*

There is not much to do here for the players, it's more of a 'exposed to Athasian culture' moment.  Most of the city is dusty and dry.  But Naggaramakam is lush and verdant.  It is the home of the Sorcerer-King and his Templar-Wives.  The players will return here later...

#### The Cracked Jar
> Tzania leads you to an unassuming, low-slung building that seems to disappear into the surroundings.  *"Sleep here..."* she says *"...not good place.  But safe.  Safe more important than good."*  
> 
> You step inside and are struck by the sounds and smells of a flophouse.  Stale, sweaty air and the sound of low, broken conversation.  A man sits at a desk near the entrance counting ceramic bits in a shallow bowl.  He lean to the point of gauntness, his skin darkened and cracked from years of sun and dust, his hair pulled back in a thinning knot bound with faded cord. One of his eyes is clouded — not blind, but damaged long ago — and it gives him a perpetually lopsided, assessing stare. He wears layered robes that were once decent but have been repaired so many times with mismatched stitching that they resemble a patchwork blanket more than clothing. His hands are steady, careful hands: the hands of a man who measures everything, especially people.
> 
> The man looks you up and down, evaluating you.  Gauging you.  Then he sees Tzania.  He sighs.  There is a sharp exchange in the strange language of this place between him and Tzania (H: "More shadows, Tzania?" T: "They'll pay.  They'll leave.  Same as always." H: "The last shadows you brought had Templars following them." T: "And you made good coin from them.  And from selling the Wives what you heard.  These aren't that type of shadow anyway.  They're strangers.  They don't speak Nibenese.  Barely speak Tyrian.  Don't know where they are from.  But they have coin - they'll pay you twice your normal rate.  And if they whisper anything that the Wives need to hear I'm sure it'll make it to the right ears, won't it?").  The man sighs again, then looks up at you.  *"No trouble?"* he says.  It's both a question and a command.  *"No beds.  Space on floor.  Roof.  Water extra.  Two bits for space on floor.  Four bits for pretend privacy..."* he gestures to a place in the back of the room where the sleeping spaces are separated by short hanging reed 'curtains'. *"...two bits for water.  Morning or night.  Not both."*

If the party asks why they are staying at a flop house Tzania will explain to them.  *"You strangers.  You no want eyes on you.  You have secrets.  You stay at a better place, people see.  People pay attention.  Ask who are strangers with coin?  Ask what you do here?  Ask why you here?  This?  No one look here.  Only wives.  Wives look, see everything.  No one ask questions.  Safer."*

##### The Invitation
> As evening settles into night the Cracked Jar settles into an uneasy quiet.  Then, just as you are settling down for the evening, the light comes.  It is not torchlight, nor it lantern light.  This clean, hard brilliance bleeds through the shutters and under the door light liquid gold.  Hushed conversations die mid sentence.  A cup is dropped, shattering on the packed earth floor.  But no one moves to pick it up.  
> 
> Then the door opens.  The light flows now, almost blinding.  The night air rushes in - hot and dry and choking, and with it step four warrior-women.  They move as one, clad in black chitinous armour polished to a mirror sheen - catching the light that wreathes them like an aura.  Each wears a skirt of deep yellow - layered silk stiffened with resin and marked by indecipherable sigils.  Each bears a long spear tipped with obsidian and gilded bindings - weapons meant as much for judgment as for war.   The light around them dose not flicker.  It does not dim.  It simply is.
> 
> Every patron of the Cracked Jar who was standing in standing no more.  Men and women and children all drop to their knees, prostrating themselves.  Some touch their foreheads to the floor.  Others tremble in terror.  The fear here is a physical thing.  Not panic, but submission.  This is not a raid.  This is not a search.  This is presence.
> 
> Hassir is out from behind the counter and flat on the ground before the four women before the door had fully opened.  He speaks to them in the strange language of this place, his voice low and steady, but with a tremor of fear.  (H: "Honoured wives of the Shadow-King.  This house is open.  It's water is yours.  It's walls hear only obedience).  One of the women steps forward, scanning the room.  She responds to Hassir without even looking at him - as if he was insignificant. (T: "Rise only if commanded").  
> 
> The warrior woman continues to scan the room.  Where she looks the air seems to tighten.  She doesn't count people.  She measures them.  And when she sees you the light subtly intensifies.  
> 
> *"He has felt you."* she says, her voice sharp, her command of common far superior to anyone else you have encountered *"You walk loudly in his city, even when you try to walk softly."*  She slams the but end of her spear into the floor.  The sound cracks like thunder.  *"You will come to Naggaramakam."*  There is no threat in her words.  None is needed.  
> 
> Hassir dares to lift his head just enough to speak again his voice tight.  (H: "Great ones, if they have offende-") but the warrior woman cuts him off sharply (T: "Silence.  They are not yours to speak for.")  Then they just stare and wait for you.

## Naggaramakam
Assuming the players do not do something stupid like try and fight the Templar Wives of Nibenay (if they do, they wind up fighting a lot of Templars as well as towns people of all sorts and hauled away unconscious and defeated - play it by ear) they are escorted by the four Templar Wives to Naggaramakam.

> You are led into the lush, grand temple-palace of Naggaramakam by your Templar-Wife escort.  The air here is cool, almost pleasant, as the stifling heat is kept at bay by thick stone walls carved in endless bas-reliefs: processions of slaves; stylized battles; lovers entwined with serpents; kings crowned by the sun.  The corridors climb and narrow, like the inside of a mountain temple, and water runs somewhere unseen, a constant trickle that echoes like a whispered breath.  The air smells here - of incense, of old stone, of something faintly metallic.
> 
> After winding through the temple-palace for some time your escort parts and gestures for you to continue forward, into a high, open chamber shaped like a stepped lotus.  The ceiling rises in tiered stone rings, each carved with nagas and celestial dancers frozen mid-motion. Shafts of moon and starlight, amplified by some means, fall from narrow apertures far above, illuminating the room in cool, silver bands. At the centre is a low stone dais and upon it rests a board of some sort - similar in design to a chess board but with a complicated, almost mind-bending geometry; the board - in lain with precious metals such as gold, silver and even mithril and platinum - moving around itself, changing even as it sits stationary.  The pieces are equal to the board; crafted from bone, obsidian, chitin, and ceramic glazed in a myriad of colours.  The pieces move on their own, gliding with soft clicks around the board, following invisible commands.
> 
> After a moment you see the figure behind the board.  He is not hiding - in fact he is sitting in plain sight, and you wonder for a moment how you missed him.  He sits on a cushioned stone bench, one leg folded beneath him the other stretching towards the board.  He is tall, unnaturally so, with a build that suggests strength held in perfect restraint. His skin is a deep bronze, smooth and unblemished, catching the light like polished metal. He wears layered robes of silk and gold thread, cut to expose his arms and throat, and his jewelry — rings, torque, and crown — is heavy with ancient authority.
> 
> At first glance, he is handsome. At second glance, unsettling.
>
