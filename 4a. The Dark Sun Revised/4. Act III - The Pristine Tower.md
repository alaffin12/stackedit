### The Bogo Forest
Traversing the bogo forest is dangerous.  It is filled to the brim with predators.  Not the large, hulking kind which the party is accustomed to facing, but the small, insidious kind that hunt and kill without being seen.  And worse yet - even if the predators do not kill, they are servants of a greater power.  Wounds fester and mutate in the Bogo Forest, and what was a simple scratch becomes something else entirely more dangerous so close to the Pristine Tower.  The Bogo Forest is a skill challenge the party must survive to reach the Pristine Tower.

### The Pristine Tower
This is where the party will learn what they need to learn to achieve their goal.  The truth of Athas is that the Rhulisti Halflings, who ruled Athas in the Blue Age, tampered with reality using the Ritual of Remaking.  In doing so they began to unravel their world altogether - unleashing a calamity they called the "Brown Tide".  

The Pristine Tower is a bulwark against this unraveling functioning in a similar way to Mordenkainen's Book of Truth, but approaching it from a completely different angle.  While Mordenkainen's plan was to used established "key" facts to anchor the cosmos, the Rhulisti Elves that built the Pristine Tower sought to create their own key facts.  This act is incredible resource intensive - ultimately killing the gods of Athas, draining magic from the world and even weakening the sun of Athas.  Later tampering with the tower (by Rajaat) exacerbated this situation.  

To find all this the party will have to ascend the tower - but this is easier said than done, as there are several obstacles in their way.  These are:

Dregoth, Xal'Thur (a Rhulisti servitor of Vecna, who has been sent by the archlich to aid and monitor Dregoth), damage to the Reality Engine (which is needed to power the elevator to ascend the final parts of the tower and reach the Chapel of Crystal) and the crystal barrier around the Chapel of Crystal.  The puzzle will be in the first three rooms (the wheel, the loom and the engine) while Xal'thur waits for the party in the engine room.  Dregoth is in the Chapel of Crystal itself, performing a profane ritual.

#### The Wheel
> The doors part before the party's hands fully touch them, two slabs of pale stone veined with crystal that dwarf even what a giant might need, swinging inward with a silence that feels deliberate—as though the Pristine Tower has been holding its breath since the world was young, waiting precisely for this moment and these footsteps. Beyond the threshold, the chamber opens into impossible space, its walls smooth and white as bone, untouched by Athas's red dust or the scouring wind that has stripped every other ruin on the planet to ash and memory. Dominating the far end, a staircase of solid crystal spirals upward into shadow, each translucent step catching light that has no visible source, refracting it into fractured rainbows that climb the walls like something alive. At the base of the stairs, worked into the floor itself, a great wheel-shaped mosaic spreads outward in concentric spokes of inlaid silver and obsidian—motionless, yet somehow suggesting rotation, as if it spins too slowly or too fast for mortal eyes to register.
> 
>It is the air above that demands the eye, though. Hanging in the chamber's center, untethered from any wall or ceiling, a churning tide of light and shadow roils and folds in on itself—a sea without water, without shore, where glimpses surface and vanish before they can be named: a city burning that has never burned, a face that might be a companion's or might be a stranger's, a door that opens onto a door that opens onto another door. This raw chaos does not simply hang there; it draws downward toward the wheel at the staircase's base, drawn into its turning as loose fiber is drawn into a spindle, and what spills out the other side is finer, thinner, almost orderly—a single bright thread of twisted potential spiraling upward around the crystal steps, climbing toward the tower's higher floors as though something above is patiently waiting to receive it. The simple beauty of it is marred by kinks and knots, twisting the string, or causing it to fray or making it too bulky to pass easily.  The party can feel the pull of it, a chaos of futures and pasts and things that almost were, being combed into something like sense. The Pristine Tower was not built to be admired. It was built to spin this—and somewhere in the heights above, where that thread of unmade reality is climbing, waits the answer to why the tower has endured while every other wonder of Athas has long since crumbled into dust.

#### The Loom
> The staircase delivers the party into a chamber narrower than the one below, its ceiling lost somewhere in a haze of refracted light, and at its heart stands the loom—if loom is even the right word for something with no visible frame, no shuttle, no weaver's hand guiding it. The bright thread that spiraled up from the wheel-room below feeds into this space through the floor itself, rising in a slow, deliberate column before it is caught and drawn taut between unseen warp-points that seem to exist only where the eye happens to fall. The thread crosses and recrosses itself in patterns too quick and too precise to follow, weaving not cloth exactly, but something that behaves like cloth: a fabric of twisted potential, shimmering faintly with colors that have no names, folding outward from the loom's center and rising toward the ceiling to vanish into the tower's higher reaches, bound for whatever waits above to receive it. A second staircase climbs from the far wall of the chamber, opposite the one the party ascended, its crystal steps catching the same fabric-light as it spirals upward into shadow.
>
>But something here is wrong, and the wrongness does not feel like the loom's own. The weave catches, almost imperceptibly, at the same interval again and again, a stutter so faint it might be missed entirely if not for the way the fabric ripples afterward, a flaw rippling outward through every later row like a stone dropped into still water that should have settled and never quite does. Threads that should cross cleanly instead hesitate, doubling back on themselves for a fraction of a heartbeat, not as though the loom were failing, but as though something foreign were pressed into the weave at precisely that interval, again and again, too deliberate to be accident. The resulting fabric, beautiful as it is, carries that wrongness forward—each new section subtly less certain than the last—and though neither loom nor thread shows any sign of what is happening, the pattern itself suggests intention: not a flaw in the machine, but a hand unseen, working against it from somewhere nearby.

#### The Engine
>The stairway opens onto a chamber unlike anything below it, vast and domed, where the fabric of woven potential pours down from a ragged seam in the ceiling and gathers around a structure at the room's exact center—the Reality Engine. It rises in lobed, folded masses of pale crystal and some softer, organic-seeming material that catches the light wetly, its surface a maze of branching grooves and convolutions that seem to fold in on themselves no matter which angle they're viewed from, threaded everywhere with thin, pulsing lines of light that flicker and chase each other like something thinking. The descending fabric feeds directly into its folds, vanishing into channels too narrow and too numerous to count, and where the engine is whole, motes of solid light drift outward from it and sink into the floor, the walls, the air itself—becoming, in some small and permanent way, real. Opposite the stairway, set into the far wall, a plain shaft housing some manner of platform-lift waits in silence, its mechanism humming faintly, offering passage upward without a single visible step.  And everywhere you look you see something familiar - sigils and runes that seem to be taken from Mordenkainen's Book of Truth.
>
>But the engine is not whole. Whole sections of its folded crystal lie fractured and dark, the lines of light within them stuttered to black, and the fabric pouring into those channels backs up and spills uselessly across the floor in shimmering, wasted pools. Crouched near the damage, inspecting his work with the satisfied patience of someone who has all the time in the world, is a Rhulisti halfling—small, sun-browned, and utterly out of place amid the ruin he's caused, a heavy tool of dark, half-organic metal still resting against the engine's broken surface. He straightens as the party enters, unhurried, regarding them with the mild curiosity of someone interrupted mid-task rather than caught in the act, and makes no move to flee. Whatever happens next, the Reality Engine will not mend itself—and every moment spent dealing with him is another moment the tower's true purpose lies broken, leaking unrealized potential across the floor.
>
>**"I had hoped it would not come to this..."*** the halfling says grimly **"...I am not a violent creature by nature.  I do not relish combat.  Combat is what remains when all other options have been exhausted.  It is a failure.  Of imagination.  Of authority.  I detest it."**  He moves slightly, gesturing to the damaged crystalline fabric weave.  **"You are too late anyway.  That sorry excuse for a mage Dregoth has entered the Chapel of Crystal.  And I have sabotaged the Reality Engine as my master bade me.  Without it there is no means of ascending any further.  Your heroics are at an end.  So turn around and leave the tower.  Enjoy the last few moments of your lives rather than spending them struggling against the inevitable."**

#### The Puzzle
Once Xal'Thur is defeated (or possibly before) the party will have to figure out how to repair the Reality Engine to reactivate the Elevator.  The engine can be activated immediately, but it should be clear to the players that the damage Xal'Thur caused has caused problems further back in the production line, and that the only way to really fix it is to start from the bottom and work their way up.  The proper restarting sequence is:

1: Fix the feed of 'reality thread' from the wheel
2: Reset the machinery in the 'reality loom' to make the 'reality fabric' come out properly.
3. Restart the 'reality engine'.

Allow the players to approach the problems however they like.  Each problem requires 4 successful skill checks to pass, and each problem will have a cost for failed checks.  It will also have a DC for poor, good and ideal skill use.  Higher floors will also get easier if lower floors are completed.

**Fixing the 'Reality Thread'**
*To fix the 'Reality Thread' has been fouled by problems in the 'reality engine' - essentially the damage done to the 'reality loom' has made it so that the the tension on the thread has gone slack, causing it to get tangled and twisted into other 'reality fiber' floating about in the room.  To fix the party must clean and repair the 'reality thread' adding fibres and thinning the thread where needed.*
DC: 17/18/19
Cost on Fail: 1d8 psychic damage (feedback from the possibilities they are weaving).

**Fixing the 'Reality Loom'**
*When the reality engine was damaged on the floor above the loom took some of the blow back.  This has resulted in the mechanical parts of the loom getting bent out of shape, or falling out of alignment.  It must be fitted back together properly for it to start working again.*
DC: 23/25/27
Cost on Fail: 5d8 force damage (pulled into the loom, trapped between reality and machinery).
If the players have fixed the feed of reality thread DCs drop by 5.

**Restarting the 'Reality Engine'**
*The damage sections of the engine must be replaced by new fabric.  Otherwise the reality engine cannot start...*
DC: 24/26/28
Cost on Fail: 5d12 random damage  (reality engine backfires)
If the players have fixed the feed of reality thread the DC drops by 1.  If the players have fixed the reality loom then the DC drops by 4.
