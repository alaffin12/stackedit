# Outline
This battle will consist of four phases (although perhaps it would be better to describe it as a role play phase and three active combat phases).  

## Phase I: Dregoth Revealed
> The final stair winds upward in silence.
>
> Gone is the low hum of the reality machinery below.  Gone is the weight of ancient purpose etched into the flawless stone of the tower.  Even your footsteps seem muffled, as though your destination is swallowing sound itself.  An odd radiance spills down the stairwell.  Not sunlight, nor firelight - not even light truly.  It is more the absence of darkness - frail, fragile and uncertain, but cleaner.  Possibility itself, guiding you, calling you forward.  
> 
> The stairs feed into a small vestibule which in turn opens into an immense, circular chamber, more than a hundred feet across.  The ceiling vanishes in to primordial shadow above, pricked with points of light.  The floor is made of the same material - darkness made solid, veined with 
> 
> The stair opens into an immense circular chamber, nearly one hundred and thirty-five feet across. Its domed ceiling vanishes into brilliance high overhead, where threads of living light drift like slow-moving constellations.
>
> At the chamber's heart rises a broad dais of polished stone. Resting within it is an enormous mechanism unlike anything any of you have ever seen. Rings of crystal, adamant, and impossibly smooth white metal rotate soundlessly around one another. Gears turn without touching. Runes ignite and extinguish in complex patterns. Every movement is impossibly precise, as though the machine itself were thinking.  Around the mechanism stretches an immense circle of luminous sigils nearly eighty feet across. Five lesser circles are woven into its outer edge, each glowing with its own impossible hue.  One burns with an icy blue.  One shines with verdant emerald.  One pulses crimson like a beating heart.  One glows violet-black, swallowing the light around it.  One blazes brilliant gold like the noonday sun.  Each circle feeds streams of arcane energy inward toward the machine.
> 
> At the cardinal points of the chamber stand four towering mirrors nearly twenty feet high. Their polished surfaces hold no reflections. Instead, they reveal shifting visions of Athas—not as it is, but as it might have been.  Vast forests.  Endless oceans.  Towering cities untouched by dust.  Then the visions ripple away.  Standing motionless upon the central dais is a lone figure.
>
> He is tall.  Regal in the way that Nibenay and the other Sorcerer Kings you have met are regal.  He wears robes of the deepest black - black enough to match walls and floor of this very room - and faded crimson, their hems embroidered with white sigils and runes that were likely old when the tower itself was young - if it ever was truly young.  A crown of black iron rests upon his head, cutting into it.  It demands your respect.  It demands your obedience.  It demands you kneel.
> 
> And then the figure turns to face you.  Beneath the robes there is no flesh.  Instead vast ribs of polished obsidian arch around a heart that burns with a cold, sapphire fire.  His face is draconic, every tooth razor sharp and the color of mithril.  Emerald witch-fire burns within his cavernous eye sockets. Great skeletal wings, stitched together of living shadow, slowly unfurl behind him.  
> 
> He does not reach for a weapon.  He does not move at all.  
>
> He watches.

Allow the players to react.  Any attempt to attack him is futile.  Dregoth, and the machine he is standing on, is shrouded in a shield made of reality itself.  Spells targeting something within the shield fail, and spells whose AoE effects would impact the region inside the field simply see the magic stop at the edge of the field.  Physical attacks cannot breath the shield (non-magical weapons actually shatter when forced against it).  Reality is bent around Dregoth.  He does exist.

When it is time Dregoth will speak to them.  His voice is calm, but authoritative.  

> **"You have climbed further than any living soul has in an age.  You have crossed the scars of the first world.  Endured the failures of those who came before.  You have seen the truth of Athas.  And you still stand."**
>  
>  He bows his head solemnly.  Not in submission, but in respect.
>
> **"You have earned the truth."**
>
> He gestures, and the mirrors blaze to life.  You see Athas transformed.  Blue oceans.  Green forests.  Clouds.  Children laughing, playing, drinking water with an abandon unknown in this place.  Cities without walls.  No slave caravans.  No arenas.  No defilers.  No templars.  No sorcerer-kings.  Only life.
>  
>  **"This is what awaits beyond the end of this age.  You have been told I seek to destroy Athas.  This is a comforting lie.  Athas is dead.  It has been dead since the Rhulisti raised this forsaken monument to their sins, to preserve themselves against a foe of their own creation."**
>
> He raises a skeletal hand.  The golden circle brightens.  The entire tower hums.
>
> **"We killed our gods to fuel this madness - and what has it left us?  The seas are gone.  The forests are ash.  Children are born only to inherit thirst and pain.  Generation after generation survives only long enough to teach the next how to endure.  You have been told I seek to destroy Athas.  This is the truth.  You seek to save it.  Tell me - what exactly is it you believe you are saving?"**
>  
>  The dais begins to move around the mechanism, carrying Dregoth with it.  **"The others cling to their power.  Hamanu.  Nibenay.  The Oba.  Every one of them speaks of preserving Athas.  Preserving the world that they rule.  They rule with an iron fist because they must.  Because Athas is cruel and hungry and demands tribute.  This device - the preservation, the salvation of this tower - demands tribute.  But not one of them has ever imagined a world where they are no longer needed.  Where this abomination is no longer needed.  I have.  I have seen it"**
>
> **"A being older than the kingdoms offered me knowledge.  Not promises.  Knowledge."**  The mirrors ignite again, displaying more impossible images.  Rain.  Birds.  Children swimming in deep pools.  A dragon soaring above forests.  **"You are not of Athas.  Perhaps these things are mundane to you.  To me they are a paradise.  He showed me this.  He showed me what Athas once was.  He showed me what Athas will become again.  You call him a monster.  Perhaps.  I have been called a monster before.  As have my brethren.  The Champions of Rajaat.  I do not know.  Perhaps we all are.  But I know this.  Monsters may still speak the truth."**
>
> **"Leave.  Take the stairs.  Tell the sorcerer-kings their age has ended.  When the work is complete there will be no slaves.  No wasteland.  No sorcerer-kings.  No hunger.  I would rather you live to see the next age.  The glory of Athas."**

Let the party debate him, if they wish.  Dregoth is intelligent, articulate and genuinely believes he is acting for the greater good.  He does not deny the horrors committed by Vecna any more than he denies the horrors committed by the sorcerer-kings.  He committed many of them himself.  Instead he sees them as failures, failures which he now seeks to transcend.

Perhaps the party will leave.  They have what they need for their book.  But allowing the Pristine Tower to fall will affect the final battle.  If they choose to interfere, however...

> Dregoth closes his eyes for a long moment.  There is regret in the action.  A pain you cannot imagine.  **"Then history repeats itself..."** he mutters as he opens them again, turning away - not dismissively, but because his attention is called elsewhere as the dais begins to move.  **"...forgive me."**
>
> As the dais moves in great arcs the remainder of the device begins to rotate and twist.  Deep within the tower something vast awakens.  Teeth carved from crystal mesh and turn and crank with impossible precision.  The five circles blaze like newborn suns.  The dark crystal, the primordial dark beneath your feet begins to vibrate.  You feel - not hear - the heartbeat of the Pristine Tower.  You feel the engine of reality turn.  It thrums with life.  And it has begun to consume itself.

## Phase II - The Tower Wakens
The party must now come to realize that Dregoth is beyond their reach.  If they attacked him at some point in phase I they might already be aware, but in phase II that effect - where the centre of the room effectively doesn't exist - continues.  This means:

1. Spells targeting something within the shield fail, and spells whose AoE effects would impact the region inside the field simply see the magic stop at the edge of the field.
2. Magical weapons used to attack the magical field result in the magical weapon resisting horribly as they strike the edge of the barrier (the PC can push the blade weapon through, but it appears somewhere else in the room.  Non-magical weapons are destroyed and if a person tries to simply walk through the edge of the magical barrier they will take 1d10 force damage and appear somewhere else in the room at random.

Once the party figures out that Dregoth is protected they have to determine how to bypass it.  The answer is, of course, disrupt the mirrors that are protecting Dregoth with this strange reality field.  But the players have to figure that out first.
