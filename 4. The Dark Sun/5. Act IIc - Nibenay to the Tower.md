### Preparations
Here the caravan slims down as it prepares to make a push through the Beast Barrens to the Tower.  The Beast Barrens are harsher than the regular desert, and consequently things will be difficult for the next seven days.  Sereth will trade the entire caravan and the slaves for 18 crodlu and 252 units of food/water (which will be enough for seven days travel at 2 units/day for each of them - the maximum amount of food/water a crodlu can carry).  For every slave that died or otherwise left camp on the journey to Nibenay remove 5 units of food/water from the provisions.  For every Templar that died or that they leave behind (or that left camp) add 10 units of food/water, but remember that this also reduces the capacity of the entire group - i.e. with one Templar missing the entire group can only carry 238 units of food/water.  Extra crodlu can be purchased by sacrificing 5 units of food/water from the total.  While the players likely have gold to supplement all this, Sereth will tell them to save it - it would not look good for agents of Urik to be seen spending so much wealth in Nibenay.  They will have time to spend it later at Fort Inix, four days away.

The DC for survival checks now increases - to DC 22 Constitution versus the heat of the day and DC 17 Constitution versus the cold of the evening.

### Day 1: The Oasis
*(after the heat check)* In the distance you see what would seem to be an unmapped oasis, shimmering in the heat of the day.  You hear whispers among your escort - some are looking off into the distance and believe that such a locale would provide a respite, while others feel that it is just a mirage - or worse, a trap.  
* **Option 1 - Investigate the oasis** It's just a mirage.  Players must make an extra heat exhaustion save today as a result of the extra effort.
* **Option 2 - Ignore** The Templars grumble, and their morale is reduced by one.
* **Option 3 - Send a small team** Two Templars (party's choice, not the High Templar) are sent out.  They do not return.

### Day 2: The Burned Sky
*(before the heat check)* In the mid-morning you notice a shimmering storm on the horizon.  It's clearly not natural, as crimson coloured lighting streaks across the sky seemingly in slow motion.  Sereth moves close to you, "Defilers..." he whispers quietly "...their magic scars the land"
* **Option 1 - Forge ahead** All arcane casters (bard, sorcerer, warlock, wizard) absorb some of the corruption and add two to their count of 'defiles'.  Additionally someone in the party must make a DC 15 Charisma skill check (let them choose their skill) to convince the other Templars to follow them.  If they fail the Templars take -2 to their morale and one Templar (chosen at random - not the High Templar) abandons the party.
*  **Option 2 - Go around** The group goes around the unnatural storm.  The group (players and Templars) must collectively give up [number of party members - Templar and PC]+2d[closest die to 1/2 of number of party members] food & water.

### Day 3 - Slaver Raid
> The desert twilight is settling in, and the by now familiar routine of setting up camp has begun.  The Templars, for all their zeal and the disdainful way they treat their slaves, are not averse to doing work as in their absence they seem to have taken over those roles naturally, leaving you - novices still unfamiliar with the way of the sands and thus little more than a burden to those more experienced that you - to relax and observe.  Fire crackle to life, packs are gathered, small shelters fashioned.  This will not be comfortable - nothing is comfortable in the desert, but it is a far cry from the weariness of the day.
>
> Then the moment of peace is shattered.  There is the rasping cry of chitin on stone as figures explode from the ridge line of the dunes - towering insect forms bounding on powerful legs, carrying nets and jagged spears that flashing in the firelight.  You hear a word coming from the Templars - thri-kreen.  Their alien clicks and hisses echo across the camp as they descend upon you in perfect coordination.
>
> You are not defenseless.  The templars react instantly: weapons drawn, shields raised, their harsh voices barking orders in a language you cannot understand.  But the thri-kreen are quick.  One Templar staggers under the weight of what looks like a simple flesh wound - a sure sign of poison.  Another is trapped in a net and hauled into the sand.  
>
> The thri-kreen mean to make slaves of you and the Templars.  You know you do not have a role to play in the formations of the Templars - they are well-drilled soldiers, used to fighting as a unit.  Together.  But you have your own skills.  They will hold the line - you will seek and find the leader of the slavers and teach him a lesson.

The nature of this battle partially depends on how many Templars travel with you.  If ten live then the raiders are mostly occupied, leaving you to fight the Thri-Kreen Captain and a Thri-Kreen Leader.  If 5-9 live then you will fight the Thri-Kreen Captain and two Thri-Kreen Leaders.  If 1-4 live then you will fight the Thri-Kreen Captain, two Thri-Kreen Leaders and 1d4 Thri-Kreen Raiders.  If none survive then the party must face the Thri-Kreen Captain, two Thri-Kreen Leaders and 6 Thri-Kreen Raiders.

If Sereth and the High Templar survive, the players will be able to call upon their aid a single time (not per player - once per surviving leader) during the encounter.

#### Sereth - Mindstorm
As a reaction, when an enemy makes a melee attack against a member of the party, they may tell Sereth to unleash a wave of psionic force.  Each enemy of Sereth's choice within 20 ft of the triggering creature must succeed on a DC 16 Intelligence save or take 6d6 psychic damage and be staggered, granting advantage on the next attack roll against them (on a success they take half damage and are not staggered).

#### High Templar - Lion's Command
As a reaction the High Templar can roar an invocation to the Lion of the Desert, filling his allies with borrowed might.  All players gain advantage on their next saving throw against exhaustion, fear or charm effects.  In addition one member of the party regains 20 hp as the Templar channels Hamanu's divine authority.

### Day 4: The Haboob
*(after the heat check)* In the afternoon you are crossing a dune when a sudden, violent dust storm kicks up, blasting your skin with razor-like sand, scouring your skin and reducing visibility to nil.  
* **Option 1 - Press forward together** The evening exhaustion save will be made at disadvantage as the party works extra hard to ensure everyone gets through the storm together.
* **Option 2 - Hunker down** The group waits out the storm, but an additional two units of food per person will have to be consumed.
* **Option 3 - Press forward at all costs** The weak are abandoned.  One Templar at random (not the high Templar) gets lost in the storm and dies.  Morale is reduced by two.

### Day 5: Fort Inix
> Your arrival at Fort Inix is met not with welcome, but with suspicion. The sandstone walls rise ahead, patched and crumbling, their once-proud banners of House Shom and Nibenay faded by sun and grit. At the gates, a knot of Shom guards in mismatched armor blocks the way, demanding to see writs of passage and levying tolls with smug certainty. Their tone makes it clear: this place is less a fortress and more a market stall, and everything has its price. The Urikite templars bristle, voices sharp with authority, insisting that Hamanu’s writ grants them safe passage on this road. The guards only laugh, spitting into the sand and declaring that Fort Inix bows to Shom’s coin and the Shadow King's hand, not Urik’s roar.

> The tension is immediate and sharp. The templars’ hands drift toward weapons, while traders and hawkers nearby watch eagerly, scenting a spectacle. Whispers ripple through the square—slaves once sold here, water priced higher than gold, the corruption of House Shom laid bare. It is in this moment, as the guards press harder for bribes, that the weight of hidden choices settles on the party.

The party has three basic choices here:
**Back the Urikite Templars:** If they are seen siding with the Urikite Templars prices will be very elevated in Fort Inix - they will have to pay 10 sp for entry, supplies (food/water) will be 5 sp each and prices in the market will be exorbitant (x2).  However the Templars will appreciate the player's support and their morale will increase by 1.

**Negotiate a Deal:** Someone in the party tries to make a deal with the guards.  DC 20 charisma skill of their choices (modify if they do a good/bad job of role playing it).  If the check is successful, entry is 5 sp, food/water is 1 sp and prices in the market will be very high will (x5).  If the check fails they piss the guards off and entry is 20 sp, food/water is 10 sp and they are banned from the market.  Templar morale drops by 1.

**Show the Symbol of Nibenay:** The party may have a symbol of Nibenay.  If they show this the Shom soldiers will let them enter for free.  Food/Water will be 5 cp and prices in the market will be elevated (x1.5).  However the Urikite Templars will be upset that the party is working with another Sorcerer-King - -2 Templar morale.

They will presumably make their way into the fort, where another moral dilemma faces them:
>The sound reaches you before the sight does: the dull crack of a whip, the bark of a trader’s call, and the low, broken cries of the desperate. As you pass through Fort Inix’s outer market, the stalls of overpriced grain and rusted tools give way to a crude wooden platform surrounded by a jeering crowd. Here, the true business of the fort reveals itself. Shackled figures are herded into the light - half-starved elves, bruised muls, even a trembling thri-kreen whose mandibles click weakly in the dust. A gaunt auctioneer, draped in the faded yellow of House Shom, shouts out bids with the rhythm of a chant, his words drowned in the clatter of bone coins and the laughter of the wealthy.

>Your Urikite Templar companions stand stiffly at the edge of the square, their expressions unreadable but their eyes sharp. Sereth merely shrugs, as if this spectacle is no different than the price of water or salt. The crowd parts slightly, and you see a young human woman pushed onto the block, her face streaked with dust and tears. She raises her eyes for a moment and meets yours. The crowd roars for her life in silver, but in that fleeting instant the square seems to fall silent—waiting to see whether you will look away, or act.

The party can choose to act (or not to act) here as they please, however as always actions have consequences:
* **Buy the Slave**: The price is exorbitant in Fort Inix, costing 300 bits (any discount earned at the gate does not apply here as this is an auction).  This results in a -1 to Templar morale as they view it as a waste of resources.  On subsequent black market purchases (non-food/water) roll 1d4.  On an even result the person they are buying from appreciates the gesture and gives them a break on prices (x0.8).  On an odd result the vendor knows the players have coin to spend, and tries to soak them (x1.2)
* **Disrupt the Auction**: House Shom is upset at the disruption.  Prices receive a 1.5x increase in the market.  The Templars are upset about having to be involved in the disruption.  -1 to Templar morale.
* **Silence**: If the party ignores the slave auction nothing happens.  Since they do not have to deal with any nonsense, Templar morale receives a +1.
* **Shadows**: If the party acts in secret to right the wrong have them roll a series of skill checks appropriate to their plan.  If they succeed then they may (or may not) reap some benefit, depending on the plan.  If they fail they are caught, have to pay a fine (30,000 bits).

### Day 6: The Singing Dune
> The wind has a voice this evening.
>
>As dusk bleeds violet across the horizon, your caravan crests a long ridge of red-gold sand. The air hums softly — a low, resonant tone that thrums through your bones and into the crodlus’ hides. Even the Templars fall silent. Before you, the dune stretches out for half a mile, smooth as glass, its surface vibrating faintly with each breath of wind.
>
>The hum deepens as you approach. The ground itself seems alive — rippling under your feet like something vast and sleeping beneath the surface. Whispers ride the wind: faint syllables in no tongue you know, fragments of song or prayer. The sound isn’t hostile, but it isn’t natural either. It feels… old. Older than the desert. Older than men, elves, or sorcerer-kings.

Buried deep beneath the dune is a rhulisti (halfling) life-shaper, who was involved in the building of the Pristine Tower in the Blue Age.  His power resonates here still, and it is amplified by the presence of outsiders like the party.  

The Templars will seem agitated by the sound.  They are not exactly superstitious, but they know that these lands hold ancient power that might be best left forgotten.  Whatever this is - they want nothing to do with it.

Sareth will be more diplomatic.  Hanuman has been generous with him, and has granted him some insight into this area.  *"It is an echo from an age long past.  Our destination is ancient - from an era before even Rajaat.  This song is from the same era."*  He will also encourage the party to take action. *"We cannot stay here as things are.  We must either march through the night and leave this place behind, or we must silence the song."*
