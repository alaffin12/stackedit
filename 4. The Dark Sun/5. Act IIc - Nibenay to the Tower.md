### Preparations
Here the caravan slims down as it prepares to make a push through the Beast Barrens to the Tower.  The Beast Barrens are harsher than the regular desert, and consequently things will be difficult for the next seven days.  Sereth will trade the entire caravan and the slaves for 18 crodlu and 252 units of food/water (which will be enough for seven days travel at 2 units/day for each of them - the maximum amount of food/water a crodlu can carry).  For every slave that died or otherwise left camp on the journey to Nibenay remove 5 units of food/water from the provisions.  For every Templar that died or that they leave behind (or that left camp) add 10 units of food/water, but remember that this also reduces the capacity of the entire group - i.e. with one Templar missing the entire group can only carry 238 units of food/water.  Extra crodlu can be purchased by sacrificing 5 units of food/water from the total.  While the players likely have gold to supplement all this, Sereth will tell them to save it - it would not look good for agents of Urik to be seen spending so much wealth in Nibenay.  They will have time to spend it later at Fort Inix, four days away.

The DC for survival checks now increases - to DC 22 Constitution versus the heat of the day and DC 17 Constitution versus the cold of the evening.

You find a small ravine, where softer stone has been worn away by the sand and wind, leaving harder stone to form high walls - less than idea tactically, as it traps you somewhat, but ideal for protecting your group from sudden storms in the night.  
>
>The Templars, for all their zeal and the disdainful way they treat their slaves, are not averse to doing work as in their absence they seem to have taken over those roles naturally, leaving you - novices still unfamiliar with the way of the sands and thus little more than a burden to those more experienced that you - to relax and observe.  Fire crackle to life, packs are gathered, small shelters fashioned.  This will not be comfortable - nothing is comfortable in the desert, but it is a far cry from the weariness of the day.
>
> Then the moment of peace is shattered.  There is the rasping cry of chitin on stone as figures explode from the ridge line of the dunes - towering insect forms bounding on powerful legs, carrying nets and jagged spears that flashing in the firelight. The nature of this battle partially depends on how many Templars travel with you.  If ten live then the raiders are mostly occupied, leaving you to fight the Thri-Kreen Captain and a Thri-Kreen Leader.  If 5-9 live then you will fight the Thri-Kreen Captain and two Thri-Kreen Leaders.  If 1-4 live then you will fight the Thri-Kreen Captain, two Thri-Kreen Leaders and 1d4 Thri-Kreen Raiders. If Sereth and the High Templar survive, the players will be able to call upon their aid a single time (not per player - once per surviving leader) during the encounter.

Each enemy of Sereth's choice within 20 ft of the triggering creature must succeed on a DC 16 Intelligence save or take 6d6 psychic damage and be staggered, granting advantage on the next attack roll against them (on a success they take half damage and are not staggered).

All players gain advantage on their next saving throw against exhaustion, fear or charm effects.  In addition one member of the party regains 20 hp as the Templar channels Hamanu's divine authority.

### Day 5: Fort Inix
> Your arrival at Fort Inix is met not with welcome, but with suspicion. The sandstone walls rise ahead, patched and crumbling, their once-proud banners of House Shom and Nibenay faded by sun and grit. At the gates, a knot of Shom guards in mismatched armor blocks the way, demanding to see writs of passage and levying tolls with smug certainty. Their tone makes it clear: this place is less a fortress and more a market stall, and everything has its price. The Urikite templars bristle, voices sharp with authority, insisting that Hamanu’s writ grants them safe passage on this road. The guards only laugh, spitting into the sand and declaring that Fort Inix bows to Shom’s coin and the Shadow King's hand, not Urik’s roar.

> The tension is immediate and sharp. The templars’ hands drift toward weapons, while traders and hawkers nearby watch eagerly, scenting a spectacle. Whispers ripple through the square—slaves once sold here, water priced higher than gold, the corruption of House Shom laid bare. It is in this moment, as the guards press harder for bribes, that the weight of hidden choices settles on the party.

The party has three basic choices here:
**Back the Urikite Templars:** If they are seen siding with the Urikite Templars prices will be very elevated in Fort Inix - they will have to pay 10 sp for entry, supplies (food/water) will be 5 sp each and prices in the market will be exorbitant (x2).  However the Templars will appreciate the player's support and their morale will increase by 1.

**Negotiate a Deal:** Someone in the party tries to make a deal with the guards.  DC 20 charisma skill of their choices (modify if they do a good/bad job of role playing it).  If the check is successful, entry is 5 sp, food/water is 1 sp and prices in the market will be very high will (x5).  If the check fails they piss the guards off and entry is 20 sp, food/water is 10 sp and they are banned from the market.  Templar morale drops by 1.

**Show the Symbol of Nibenay:** The party may have a symbol of Nibenay.  If they show this the Shom soldiers will let them enter for free.  Food/Water will be 5 cp and prices in the market will be elevated (x1.5).  However the Urikite Templars will be upset that the party is working with another Sorcerer-King - -2 Templar morale.

They will presumably make their way into the fort, where another moral dilemma faces them:
>The sound reaches you before the sight does: the dull crack of a whip, the bark of a trader’s call, and the low, broken cries of the desperate. As you pass through Fort Inix’s outer market, the stalls of overpriced grain and rusted tools give way to a crude wooden platform surrounded by a jeering crowd. Here, the true business of the fort reveals itself. Shackled figures are herded into the light - half-starved elves, bruised muls, even a trembling thri-kreen whose mandibles click weakly in the dust. A gaunt auctioneer, draped in the faded yellow of House Shom, shouts out bids with the rhythm of a chant, his words drowned in the clatter of bone coins and the laughter of the wealthy.

>Your Urikite Templar companions stand stiffly at the edge of the square, their expressions unreadable but their eyes sharp. Sereth merely shrugs, as if this spectacle is no different than the price of water or salt. The crowd parts slightly, and you see a young human woman pushed onto the block, her face streaked with dust and tears. She raises her eyes for a moment and meets yours. The crowd roars for her life in silver, but in that fleeting instant the square seems to fall silent—waiting to see whether you will look away, or act.

The party can choose to act (or not to act) here as they please, however as always actions have consequences:
* **Buy the Slave**: The price is exorbitant in Fort Inix, costing 300 bits (any discount earned at the gate does not apply here as this is an auction).  This results in a -1 to Templar morale as they view it as a waste of resources.  On subsequent black market purchases (non-food/water) roll 1d4.  On an even result the person they are buying from appreciates the gesture and gives them a break on prices (x0.8).  On an odd result the vendor knows the players have coin to spend, and tries to soak them (x1.2)
* **Disrupt the Auction**: House Shom is upset at the disruption.  Prices receive a 1.5x increase in the market.  The Templars are upset about having to be involved in the disruption.  -1 to Templar morale.
* **Silence**: If the party ignores the slave auction nothing happens.  Since they do not have to deal with any nonsense, Templar morale receives a +1.
* **Shadows**: If the party acts in secret to right the wrong have them roll a series of skill checks appropriate to their plan.  If they succeed then they may (or may not) reap some benefit, depending on the plan.  If they fail they are caught, have to pay a fine (30,000 bits).

### Day 6: The Singing Dune
> The wind has a voice this evening.
>
>As dusk bleeds violet across the horizon, your caravan crests a long ridge of red-gold sand. The air hums softly — a low, resonant tone that thrums through your bones and into the crodlus’ hides. Even the Templars fall silent. Before you, the dune stretches out for half a mile, smooth as glass, its surface vibrating faintly with each breath of wind.
>
>The hum deepens as you continue. The ground itself seems alive — rippling under your feet like something vast and sleeping beneath the surface. Whispers ride the wind: faint syllables in no tongue you know, fragments of song or prayer. The sound isn’t hostile, but it isn’t natural either. It feels… old. Older than the desert. Older than men, elves, or sorcerer-kings.

Buried deep beneath the dune is a rhulisti (halfling) life-shaper, who was involved in the building of the Pristine Tower in the Blue Age.  His power resonates here still, and it is amplified by the presence of outsiders like the party.  

The Templars will seem agitated by the sound.  They are not exactly superstitious, but they know that these lands hold ancient power that might be best left forgotten.  Whatever this is - they want nothing to do with it.

Sareth will be more diplomatic.  Hanuman has been generous with him, and has granted him some insight into this area.  *"It is an echo from an age long past.  Our destination is ancient - from an era before even Rajaat.  This song is from the same era."*  He will also encourage the party to take action. *"We need to rest, but we cannot stay here as things are.  We must either march through the night and leave this place behind, or we must silence the song."*

#### Choices and Consequences
##### Silence the Song
Allow the party to come up with some way to disrupt the psionic song, allow skill checks (and assistance from Sereth) as needed.  When they finally succeed:

To say they have faces is wrong - for moments you feel you can see fragments of screaming halflings, fossilized in psionic anguish.  But those moments are fleeting, and the 'faces' horrifying. If they succeed they have advantage on the night's saves and have the Templar's gain morale (+1).  Also grant them a boon in the tower.

#### 4. The Core
>The corridor opens into a vast, square chamber whose heart has been hollowed into a yawning void. Suspended impossibly in the centre of that emptiness floats a massive crystal prism, its faces turning with slow, deliberate grace. On either side of the gulf, ancient psi-tech emitters pulse with faint light, feeding steady streams of psionic energy into the prism. The refracted beams scatter like spectral rain, painting the walls in drifting motes of light and flickering tableaux. Some shimmer as moving constellations, tracing the arcs of stars long dead; others take the form of fragmented memories — half-seen figures, alien landscapes, or flashes of towering white spires beneath a blue sun. The air hums faintly, and as the prism turns, the party feels their thoughts being pulled toward it, as though the crystal itself remembers them — or is searching for something it has lost.

At this point characters with latent psychic ability (at time or writing Mazirak and Emila) begin to hear psionic whispers emanating from the core.  The words will be garbled and and unintelligible, like cross talk on a radio, but they will be there.  Both non-psionic and psionic characters see various images - just as garbled as the whisper - appear, repeat, then vanish again.  If the players want they can make a check of an appropriate skill to see if they can decipher the purpose of this machine:

| Roll | Revealed |
| -- | -- |

| 16+  | The device seems to use psionic energy to achieve this. |
| 19+ | Most of the data seems to be corrupt.  What remains seems to be recordings of key events |
| 22+ | The device does not store data.  It measures perturbances in psionic energy from celestial bodies, which are caused by significant events and can be used to recreate the events.  The reason why much of the data seems corrupt is that a significant event has thrown the stars out of alignment, therefore only some data can be accessed and recreated. |
| 25+ | In theory, in the past, this setup could've been used to peer into the future too. |

Once they understand what the core is (or rather, once they have some understanding of the core) they might try to investigate it.  There are lots of ways to do that.  Let the players choose skills and try again - however this time is more risky as they are tampering with psi-tech.  Base DC is 20 with advantage for clever skill use and disadvantage for bad (but not impossible) ones.  A success results in one scene playing out.  A failure forces the failed PC to make a DC 18 Intelligence saving throw, dealing 5d8 psychic damage and giving the failed PC disadvantage on one type of saves (determined at random) until their next short or long rest on a failure.  On a success they reveal one of three pieces of lore...

##### Scene 1: The Birth of the Brown Tide
> A pulse of energy fills the room as the image swirls around, rapidly rearranging itself.  Tiny dots move around the crystal of the core and an image, fragile and brittle, cast from raw light and tinged purple, appears.  You see and image of shimmering ocean of azure light. Shapes swim beneath the surface - small, radiant beings, halfling-like but flawless and alien in grace. Around them rise coral spires of living crystal, their surfaces pulsing with mathematical beauty. The scene shivers, and the sea darkens. The light thickens into a brown, suffocating mass, a living blight consuming everything. You hear voices — proud, then desperate — cursing for the some rite, then screaming as their world dissolves into sludge. The water collapses into dust, leaving only silence and a faint echo: *“We sought to remake perfection.”*

PC's watching the scene can make one of the following checks, revealing certain information if they succeed:
* **Arcana (DC 21):** The corruption you see arising from the see and destroying the world is not natural.  It is an arcane backlash against a potent spell.
* **Insight (DC 18):** The halfling-like creature you see are arrogant.  You get the sense that the creators believe that whatever created the corruption was a necessary correction to creation
*  **Perception (DC 20):** Some of the sigils and shapes you see are similar to other ones you have seen in the Book of True Balance.

##### Scene 2: The Creation of the Pristine Tower
> The chamber fills with blinding white light — you see an image of a tower rising, slender and gleaming like glass, its base surrounded by shimmering halflings channeling psionic energy from vast crystal conduits. The landscape burns away as light pours from the tower’s apex, transforming sea into stone, sky into fire, and life into new, unfamiliar forms. You see creatures crawling from the dust, changed, gasping, surviving. The light dims, revealing a desolate world — not dead, but wounded. A voice whispers, layered and weary: *“We saved the world… by breaking it.”*

PC's watching this scene can make one of the following check, revealing more information if they succeed:
* **Arcana (DC 20):** The tower is not the source of the energy it is emitting.  It's hard to describe, but it's like spell-casting implements and material components - when a wizard casts a spell they channel the energy through these things to shape the spell.  In this case the tower seems like it is drawing the energy into itself and using it to radically alter the world.  
* **Nature (DC 19):** The light of the tower is powerful, but unfocused.  It sweeps over the land, changing it in radical ways you can scarcely comprehend.  It doesn't clean the corruption of the Brown Tide.  It changes it.  And everything around it.
* **Religion (DC 18):** There is something more here.  Something greater.  The Pristine Tower changed the world, but it also cut it off from something greater.

##### Scene 3: Rajaat and Vecna
> The light begins to spin wildly out of control, as a cacophony of images and sounds are all emitted - as if the device is trying to show you everything at once.  You can't make out any of what you are seeing or hearing, bar one detail.  Each of you hear your own name.  In a voice that is not of the Rhulisti.  And then, suddenly, darkness...
>
> After a moment is inky blackness an image springs to life.  A lone figure, tall and shadowed, haloed by the same light you once saw come from the tower.  You see him wielding something - not a weapon, but a device.  It further focuses the light of the tower.  You see sixteen figures surround the lone figure.  He changes them with the device - just like the world was changed, but focused on individuals.  You recognize two of the forms - Hamanu and Nibenay.  The others you don't know.  As he changes them you hear whispering words twist and shift: "restore...return...unmake" 
>
> The scene then fractures, flying apart back into the cacophony - quiet now, but just as incomprehensible.  In the last image you can recognize the a massive skeletal hand reaches through reality itself as the tower bleeds light.  Then nothing.

PC's watching this scene can make one of the following check, revealing more information if they succeed:
* **Arcana (DC 22):** There is a link between the lone figure's actions in creating the individuals and the actions of the Rhulisti in creating the Brown Tide.  Almost as if he wanted to continue with the Rhulisti experiments that created the Brown Tide, but do so in a way that avoided the calamity it caused.  The signs and sigils used by the lone figure are the same as used by the Rhulisti - and you have seen them elsewhere in the Book of True Balance.
* **Insight (DC 21):** The lone figure is obsessed with his task.  The Rhulisti in the first visions were greedy.  The lone figure is greedy too, but he is motivated with a desire to restore what was lost.
* **Perception (DC 20):** The runes and sigils used are familiar to you.  They appear in the Book of True Balance.  And the skeletal hand can only mean one thing - Vecna...

Once all three scenes are shown the light in the core goes out.  

#### 1. Refracted Memories
> The corridor opens into this chamber, its walls and floor carved from a smooth, pale stone that hums faintly with psionic resonance. Six crystal slabs stand around the room. Their surfaces shimmer with a cold inner light, like frozen starlight caught in ice.  As you step across the threshold, a deep thrumming sound ripples through the air. The crystals brighten in sequence—one after another—until the room is awash in refracted color. Then, one by one, images bloom across the slabs’ surfaces.
>
> You see yourselves.
>
> Each image is a frail, lifelike reflection of a party member.  And each of you remembers exactly the scene shown.  These are not reflections.  These are memories.  More than that the scene you see plays out over and over again.  And they are not quite right.  They are not only memories.  They are memories of both what was and what might have been.

Player have an opportunity to interact with these refracted memories.  Each of them will see a different scene that they have to deal with.  Dealing with what they see is subjective, but allow them leeway (it is their characters after all) forcing them to make checks or saves as they go or at the end to put it all together (with the DC determined by their roleplay).  If they succeed then they will gain the following boon:

**Boon of Reflection:** You have looked at yourself and your past and have come to terms with it.  Once per long rest when you fail a Wisdom saving throw you may choose to roll again and take the higher result.
