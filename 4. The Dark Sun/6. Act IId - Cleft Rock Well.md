## Cleft Rock Well Cavern General Description
### Appearance/Feel
The party has stumbled into an ancient research station of the Rhulisti.  The Rhulisti civilization was far beyond that of Athas today and they used a union of magic, psionics and powerful technologies to dominate the world in a manner beyond all modern understanding.  Once the party moves into the actual research station from the cavern (the collapsed entrance of the research station, now reopened) it should feel like something out of scifi - as if the Rhulisti willed the research station into existence (which they did) and shaped everything with perfect thought.  Surfaces are smooth, utilities are advanced - think Star Trek meets magic.  It should feel organic and futuristic, even though the Rhulisti civilization existed far in the past.  Rooms are about ten feet high except as noted.

This facility was a prototype of the Pristine Tower.  While the Pristine Tower draws energy from all life, the facility draws in the life force of those inside it, and the drain grows the more people inside it. ### 1. Hall of Mirrors
>Stepping through the ragged break in the stone cavern feels like slipping across an impossible threshold. The rough walls of Athas fall away behind you, and the atmosphere seems to tighten—air pressure shifting, sound dampening—as if the space itself is aware of your presence. The floor here is a single seamless surface of pale, nacreous material, smooth as polished bone but warm underfoot, as though something deep beneath it still lives. The ten-foot walls curve upward with a sculpted precision that is neither carved nor built; it feels grown through deliberate will. Every surface bears the immaculate, organic perfection of Rhulisti craft.
>
>The room stretches long to the north, a quiet cathedral of impossible geometry. Along each side wall stand tall, slightly convex panels that at first seem like mirrors—though the more you look, the less certain that feels. Their surfaces ripple faintly, like liquid held perfectly vertical. Each mirror is framed in living matter: braided ridges of soft-glowing strands that pulse slowly, like the breathing of some slumbering creature. The light they cast is indirect and source-less, shimmering between colours your mind can name and others it was never meant to perceive.
>
>Whenever you catch your reflection in one of the mirrors, the surface stirs. The image fractures, multiplies, shifts. You see yourself as you are… then as you were long ago… then as a stranger wearing your face but marked with scars of choices you have not yet made. Whispered voices slip across the room in a language that is not spoken but felta storm of impressions brushing the edge of your thoughts, calling to you: what is, what was, what will be, and what must never be. Each whisper is as gentle as falling sand, yet it lingers like an omen you almost understand.
>
>Shadows behave strangely here. They stretch at odd angles, bending toward certain mirrors, recoiling from others. Light seems to originate not from fixtures but from the psi-tech itself -colourless illumination radiating through the walls like bio-luminescent marrow.
>
>At the far northern end of the chamber, a perfectly symmetrical archway grows seamlessly out of the wall, opening into a narrow hallway beyond. Its interior glows with a steady pulse, as though responding to your nearness. Behind you to the south, the cavern yawns open—rough, broken stone now feeling impossibly crude compared to the Rhulisti perfection around you, as though the world outside were a half-finished sketch and _this_ place the true reality.
>
>This is a room built by minds that shaped their environment with a thought… and it remembers.

### 2. Rhulisti Laboratory
> Crossing the next threshold feels like stepping into the exposed mind of a civilization that no longer exists. The doorway seals behind you with a soft, organic sigh, and the air changes again—cooler now, tinged with the faint sterility of alchemical vapors and something subtler, like the psychic aftertaste of ancient concentration.
> 
>The room is broad and high, its 48-by-55-foot space shaped into an oval-edged rectangle with walls that arc slightly inward, like the interior of some sculpted shell. The surfaces gleam with a soft, pearlescent sheen—always clean, as though the room quietly rejects dust and decay.
>
>Along the north and south walls stretch lines of Rhulisti instruments: benches that grow seamlessly from the floor, crystalline interfaces arranged like constellations, and arrays of softly humming devices whose purposes exist beyond modern comprehension. Transparent cylinders rise from the counters like captured droplets of water, swirling with suspended motes that glow whenever someone draws near. Thin vein-like filaments pulse within the walls, feeding these machines with unseen power. Where Athasian laboratories rely on heat, solvents, and physical reagents, the devices here manipulate thought, light, and something deeper—concepts distilled into tools.
>
>At the center of the chamber, carved directly into the floor’s living material, lie three great circles—each a lattice of fine geometric lines interwoven with shifting sigils. They are not merely inscribed; they _move_. The circles rotate gently, each at its own slow pace, counter-turning like vast gears beneath a translucent surface. Their movements are mesmerizing but somehow wrong—jerking subtly, hitching, as though they are attempting to align with one another and failing. Every few moments a faint tremor quivers through the floor, as if the room is trying to remember a function it once performed effortlessly.
>
>Occasionally the circles flare with a faint inner light—pale violet, then blue-gray, then something like the absence of color altogether—and a whisper of displaced air tugs at your clothing. The magic and psionic energies here feel tangled, caught in a feedback loop of half-realized activation. You sense a tension in the space, like a chord stretched tight and waiting for resolution.
>
>On the southern end of the eastern wall sits the room’s only entrance: the quiet organic portal from which you entered. It seems smaller now behind you, as though the laboratory has expanded subtly in your perception. The doorway’s frame pulses once, faintly, as if acknowledging your presence—or warning you.
>
>This chamber once supported experiments rooted in thought, will, and the reshaping of reality itself. Now it slumbers in discord, its wonders misaligned, its purpose half-forgotten but far from dormant.
