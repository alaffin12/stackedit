When the part returns from Athas, the sanctum will have documentation for the next destination for the party - an entry from one of Mordenkainen's journals

> Krynn.
>
>I find myself turning my attention to that troubled world (are not all worlds troubled though), and in particular to the events surrounding what its inhabitants call the Cataclysm. A curious business. The gods, so it is said, abandoned the world after a mortal priest grew so arrogant that he sought to place himself above them. A familiar enough tale, really. Mortals reach too far, the gods take offense, and everyone else pays the price. One could be forgiven for thinking that the gods might have found a less destructive means of making their displeasure known.
>
> But I suspect there is more to it than that. There usually is.
> 
> I have a contact there, Dalamar the Dark.  Like me, he enjoys voyaging the planes.  We meet at Ed's occasionally, exchange stories and drink tea and the like.  Through him I know of another.  One who at least claims to possess the knowledge that I seek.  Raistlin Majere.  Unlike Dalamar I find Raistlin difficult.  He has a tendency to regard every other mage in the room as either an obstacle, or inferior.  Admittedly, I have been accused of much the same, but I like to think I have the good sense to be charming about it.
> 
> In any case, I respect the man and his skills.  He has peered into places of magic and divinity that would give most archmages pause, and his understanding of Krynn is considerable. Dalamar is a student of his, and Dalamar is no slouch in the magic department himself.  That Raistlin is the master and Dalamar is the student speaks volumes about Raistlin even without saying a word.
> 
> I spoke to Raislin.  I told him of the situation.  It's dire nature.  He told me his tale.  What he knows.  I wrote it in my book.  It would not take. The ink remains, the words are legible, but the tome rejects the knowledge all the same. It is not that Raistlin is lying to me.  Dalamar assures me the man is many things, but a fool is not one of them - and he would be a fool to try and hinder this plan.  In truth I suspect I am missing something fundamental.  Perhaps it is not the Cataclysm that is the pillar.  Perhaps it is something about the Cataclysm that one must know of.  Were the gods preserving something?  Was it about the gods at all?
> 
> I must return to Raistlin.  I must probe what he knows.  He will hate the suggestion that he does not know something.  That something has escaped his notice.  No matter - I must find the deeper truth.

### Arcane Magic
Arcane magic (that which is used by wizard, sorcerer, warlock and bards) is tied to the moons of Krynn.  Mages of a good alignment are tied to the white moon (Solinari).  Mages of a neutral alignment are tied to a red moon (Lunitari).  Mages of an evil alignment are tied to a black moon (Nuitari) that is only visible to them.  The moons have the following periods (changed from canon as it is unlikely players will remain in Krynn long enough to experience them otherwise):

Solinari - 20 days (each quarter is five days long).
Lunitari - 8 days (each quarter is two days long)
Nuitari - 12 days (each quarter is three days long)

Roll 1d20, 1d8 and 1d12 to figure out what day each moon is on in its cycle and then calculate what phase (quarter) it is in, to check for effects on spell-casting:

| Phase | Effect |
| -- | -- |
| Low Sanction (New Moon) | -1 to spell attacks and save DCs
| Waxing | No change 
| High Sanction (Full Moon) | +1 to spell attacks and save DCs
| Waning | No change
