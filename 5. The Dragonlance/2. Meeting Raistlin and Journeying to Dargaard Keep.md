# Journey to Krynn
> You step into the Orrey of the Spheres and the device activates, whirling brightly around you as reality begins to dissolve and reform.  You focus it on one name.  Raistlin Majere.  The name echoes through the cosmos, and towards the realm of Krynn, drawing the orrey and you with it.  Around you you see the image of the mage form.  You have met him.  In the first battle in Sigil.  With golden skin and hourglass eyes.  Then it blinks.  The image shifts.  Something darker.  An ancient, withered figure, with eyes that burn of malevolence.  You feel the fire in his eyes.  His heart.  It burns you...
>
> And then you step into a clearing in the woods, near the ruins of an ancient stone shrine.  Nearby a young man - the mage, from the image, Raistlin Majere, sits against a stone pillar, robed in red and reading a book by torch light.  
>
> "Curious..." he says, without looking up "...I requested solitude.  It seems I was misheard."  He looks up at the party, his hour glass eyes shimmering in the moon light.  "Who are you.  And why have you intruded upon my study?"

Raistlin is a rather haughty mage, but not without cause.  He regards others as fools, calls them that to their faces, and has little time for emotional nonsense and hope.  He is a man of study, of focus and of will.  

The players will likely speak to him on a wide variety of topics.  Here are a handful of prepared responses:

#### On Mordenkainen
> The mention of the name Mordenkainen causes the red robed wizard to freeze a second.  "Mordenkainen..." he repeats the name, almost tasting it, closing his eyes and savouring it "...that is peculiar."

If the party further questions him:

> "I do not know the name.  And yet I remember an argument.  A disagreement about the nature of magic.  An extraordinarily irritating wizard - arrogant, self-interested and given to lengthy explanations of matters that were obvious to anyone with the basest of intellects.  Rather like myself I suppose.  It is an odd sensation.  To both know and not know something.  Who was this man?"

If the party further explain Mordenkainen to him, with some explanation of what happened.  

> "Curious.  Temporal magic is not a trivial thing.  I suppose that much is evident, given what has befallen this Mordenkainen.  But still, I find it intensely disagreeable that someone has modified my memory without my knowledge or consent.  I will have to do something about that."

#### On the fact he does not seem to know the truth
> "Preposterous.  You, outsiders from a realm beyond Krynn, arrive in my world uninvited, moved here by some device that allows you to hop through realms.  And you tell me that *my* knowledge of *my* realm is insufficient.  That my knowledge of Krynn is incomplete.  It is preposterous.  Insulting even."  Raistlin pauses, his face a mix of irritation, curiosity and compliance  "But that device - the one which brought you to me.  I felt its power before you even arrived.  It is a masterpiece of the arcane.  That means I will listen to you.  For a moment.  Even if you are insulting me." 

#### On the gods role in the Cataclysm
> "Spare me the dull theology.  I do not know the gods of other realms.  But I know the gods of Krynn.  More intimately than most, I suspect. They speak of balance, of harmony, of the proper ordering of things. Yet they permit suffering on a scale that would make even the most depraved mortal blush.  I know of Paladine and his precious balance.  I know of Takhisis and her endless ambition.  And I know the truth - that they play games with mortal lives, moving pieces on the board.  We suffer.  We die.  But we are nothing to them.  You suggest that there was a reason behind the Cataclysm?  That the gods were serving some greater purpose?  Or perhaps that it was something else at play?  Convenient, that the gods might hide the dark age they unleashed on us with a 'reason' that us mortals may not know."

#### When he finally gives up and acknowledges his own limits
> "I will never claim perfection.  I am the greatest.  The most deserving.  The most powerful.  But that does not make me perfect.  You speak of things beyond Krynn.  Of truths and tomes and a creature named Vecna who can threaten even the callous gods.  Very well.  I accept it as so.  You say you need knowledge, that my knowledge has been tested and found wanting.  I find such an idea irritating, but do you know what I find more irritating?  The thought that you might be right and I might never know it."
> >
> "I have studied the Cataclysm.  Istar.  The Kingpriest.  The folly of mortals.  The callous wrath of the gods.  I have seen things that would otherwise seem impossible.  I know why the gods decided to punish us.  I know why they reduced Istar to rubble.  I know of the warnings, and how they were ignored, and the dire consequences.  But I was not there.  Perhaps I have been mislead, or perhaps something has been hidden from me. "
> 
> Raistlin's face betrays a hint of distaste.  "There is one creature who may hold the truth you seek.  Soth.  He was once a man.  A man of consequence and influence.  Loren Soth.  Knight of the Rose.  Lord of Dargaard Keep.  But his own weakness and vanity undid him.  A tired tale of love, heirs and the failing virtue of knights.  Soth killed his wife, bedded a new and beautiful young wife who was 

Lord Soth.  Fallen Knight of the Rose.  The Lord of Dargaard Keep.  Once a man of of consequence before his own vanity and weakness condemned him to an eternity of undeath."  He shakes his head "Soth was there, at least in the histories that I have read.  The story goes that he had already fallen from grace.  Betrayed his love and killed her, so that he could wed another.  Mishakal offered him redemption - if he stopped the Kingpriest and ended his machinations.  And he failed."
>
> Raistlin shakes his head.  "I have read every tale, every treatise, every prophecy and every review of the Cataclysm.  I know all that has been put to paper on the topic.  But if there is one creature that might know more, that might hide secrets which have never been put to paper - it is Soth.  He alone spoke with the gods.  He alone knew their divine will, what they planned to do if he succeeded.  What they said they'd do if he failed."
>
> Raistlin pauses and shakes his head again.  "I would **not** ask him politely, however.  Dargaard Keep is not a place for visitors.  Soth has little regard for the living, and those who enter his domain without invitation have a tendency to remain there long after their natural lives should have ended.  I understand he has amassed quite an army of the dead. Perhaps he will find you useful.
